### (a)

#### Major behavioral science disciplines that contribute to Organizational Behavior

Organizational Behavior (OB) is an applied behavioral science built on contributions from a number of behavioral disciplines, mainly psychology, social psychology, sociology, and anthropology. Their contributions range from the micro-level analysis of individual dynamics to macro-level concepts regarding group processes and organizational systems.

- **Psychology:** This discipline seeks to measure, explain, and sometimes change the behavior of humans. Its contributions to OB are primarily at the **individual or micro level** of analysis.
  - _Key Areas:_ Learning, motivation, personality, emotions, perception, training, leadership effectiveness, job satisfaction, individual decision-making, performance appraisals, attitude measurement, employee selection, work design, and job stress.
- **Social Psychology:** A branch of psychology that blends concepts from both psychology and sociology, focusing on the influence of people on one another. Its contributions occur at the **group level** of analysis.
  - _Key Areas:_ Behavioral change, attitude change, communication, group processes, group decision-making, power, conflict, and intergroup behavior.
- **Sociology:** While psychology focuses on the individual, sociology studies people in relation to their social environment or culture. It contributes to OB at both the **group and organizational system level**.
  - _Key Areas:_ Group dynamics, design of work teams, organizational culture, formal organization theory and structure, organizational technology, communications, power, conflict, and intergroup behavior.
- **Anthropology:** This is the study of societies to learn about human beings and their activities. Anthropologists' work on cultures and environments has helped OB understand differences in fundamental values, attitudes, and behaviors across different countries and organizations.
  - _Key Areas:_ Comparative values, comparative attitudes, cross-cultural analysis, organizational culture, and organizational environments.

### (b)

#### Challenges and opportunities managers have in applying OB concepts

The business environment is constantly evolving, presenting managers with critical challenges and unique opportunities that require the application of Organizational Behavior concepts to improve organizational effectiveness.

- **Responding to Economic Pressures:** During economic downturns, managers face the difficult tasks of managing layoffs, maintaining employee morale when rewards are scarce, and resourcefully doing more with less. OB tools help managers understand how to motivate employees and secure commitment during stressful economic times.
- **Responding to Globalization:** Organizations are no longer constrained by national borders. Managers must navigate:
  - _Increased Foreign Assignments:_ Managing workforces with distinct cultural needs.
  - _Working with People from Different Cultures:_ Adapting leadership and communication styles to accommodate diverse cultural orientations.
  - _Overseeing Movement of Jobs to Countries with Low-Cost Labor:_ Balancing organizational efficiency with ethical and corporate social responsibilities.
- **Managing Workforce Diversity:** Workforce diversity acknowledges that organizations are becoming more heterogeneous in terms of gender, age, race, ethnicity, sexual orientation, and physical abilities. The challenge is to accommodate diverse groups by addressing their distinct lifestyles, family needs, and clinical variations, while the opportunity lies in leveraging varied perspectives to foster innovation.
- **Improving Customer Service:** Today, the majority of employees work in service jobs where substantial interaction with customers occurs. OB concepts guide managers in building customer-responsive cultures—where employees are friendly, accessible, responsive, and capable of managing emotional labor effectively.
- **Working in Networked Organizations:** Modern technology allows people to communicate and work together even if they are thousands of miles apart. Managers must adapt their skills to lead, motivate, and manage performance virtually, ensuring collaboration remains strong despite a lack of physical presence.
- **Enhancing Employee Well-Being at Work:** The boundary between work and personal life has blurred due to 24/7 global operations and mobile communications. Managers face the challenge of helping employees navigate work-life conflicts, implementing flexible work schedules, and creating environments that prevent burnout.
- **Creating a Positive Work Environment:** Managers are leveraging _Positive Organizational Scholarship_ (or positive OB), which studies how organizations develop human strengths, foster vitality, and unlock potential. This shifts the focus away from what is wrong with employees to what is right with them.

### (c)

#### How organizations manage diversity effectively

Effective diversity management makes everyone more aware of and sensitive to the needs and differences of others. It integrates comprehensive corporate practices to leverage the full potential of a varied workforce. Organizations achieve this through a multi-tiered approach:

- **Attracting, Selecting, Developing, and Retaining Diverse Employees:**
  - _Recruitment:_ Targeting recruiting messages to specific demographic groups that are underrepresented in the workforce, and partnering with diverse professional associations.
  - _Selection:_ Ensuring that hiring materials, tests, and interview protocols are highly objective, structured, and free of cultural or gender biases.
  - _Development & Retention:_ Creating transparent, merit-based career advancement tracks and implementing formal mentoring programs to support minoritized or underrepresented employees.
- **Diversity in Groups:** \* Managers form teams that leverage diverse perspectives to enhance problem-solving and creative output.
  - To mitigate the risk of faultlines (subgroup splits within a team), managers emphasize common overarching goals, build high levels of psychological safety, and foster a team culture where distinct individual experiences are valued as institutional assets.
- **Implementing Comprehensive Diversity Programs:** Effective organizations design long-term, ongoing diversity training initiatives that emphasize three critical components:
  1. _Legal Awareness:_ Educating managers and employees about the legal framework of equal employment opportunity and encouraging fair treatment regardless of demographic characteristics.
  2. _Market Benefits:_ Educating the workforce on how a diverse employee base is better equipped to understand, reach, and serve a diverse customer marketplace.
  3. _Personal Practices:_ Fostering personal development practices that bring out the unique skills, abilities, and perspectives of all workers, highlighting how differences can improve organizational performance.
