### (a)

#### Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)

The **Global Reporting Initiative (GRI)** is an international, independent network organization founded in 1997 (in partnership with the United Nations Environment Programme) that pioneers the standardization of sustainability reporting. It provides the world's most widely used framework for corporate social responsibility (CSR) disclosure, known as the **GRI Standards**.

- **Core Objective:** GRI enables businesses, governments, and non-governmental organizations to understand, measure, and communicate their impacts on critical ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) issues such as climate change, human rights, governance, and social well-being.
- **The Modular Structure of GRI Standards:**
  - _Universal Standards:_ Apply to all reporting organizations, guiding them on disclosing contextual information and managing material topics.
  - _Sector Standards:_ Focus on high-impact sectors (e.g., oil and gas, agriculture) to improve reporting quality and comparability within specific industries.
  - _Topic Standards:_ Provide specific indicators and metrics for reporting on specific material impacts (e.g., waste, occupational health and safety, anti-corruption).
- **Significance in CSR Performance:** GRI shifts corporate reporting from purely financial metrics to a **Triple Bottom Line** perspective (People, Planet, Profit). By establishing standard, quantifiable disclosure templates, GRI drives accountability, minimizes greenwashing, and allows stakeholders to benchmark and compare the authentic sustainability performance of organizations globally.

### (b)

#### Business for Social Responsibility (BSR)

**Business for Social Responsibility (BSR)** is a global non-profit organization and business network founded in 1992. Unlike standard-setting bodies, BSR functions primarily as a sustainable business consultancy, catalyst, and collaborative platform for its network of more than 300 member companies and partners worldwide.

- **Core Objective:** BSR works directly with multi-national corporations to develop, refine, and execute corporate sustainability strategies that integrate commercial performance with environmental stewardship and social equity.
- **Key Pillars of Action:**
  - _Strategic Consulting:_ Advising C-suite executives on incorporating climate resilience, human rights due diligence, and ethical supply chain metrics into their core corporate models.
  - _Research and Insights:_ Publishing cutting-edge research regarding emerging trends in technological ethics, systemic economic inequalities, and environmental challenges.
  - _Collaborative Initiatives:_ Convening cross-sector coalitions to solve large-scale systemic issues that an individual company cannot resolve alone (e.g., clean cargo working groups, sustainable supply chain networks).
- **Significance in CSR Performance:** BSR acts as an implementation catalyst. It transforms abstract CSR principles into actionable, operational reality for corporate entities, emphasizing that sustainable business practices are essential for long-term risk management and value creation.

### (c)

#### Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)

The **Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)** is an intergovernmental economic organization consisting of 38 member countries, established in 1961 to stimulate economic progress and world trade. In the context of CSR, the OECD represents a powerful institutional voice through its specialized policies and frameworks on **Responsible Business Conduct (RBC)**.

- **Core Objective:** The OECD aims to foster open, rules-based international economies while ensuring that multinational enterprises align their operations with sustainable development and societal progress.
- **The OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises:** This is the most comprehensive set of government-backed recommendations on responsible business conduct in existence. Adhered to by both member and non-member countries, these guidelines cover key CSR performance landscapes:
  - _Human & Labor Rights:_ Respecting trade unions, eliminating forced/child labor, and preventing discrimination.
  - _Environmental Management:_ Implementing precautionary approaches and environmental safety mechanisms.
  - _Anti-Corruption & Taxation:_ Combatting bribery, extortion, and promoting transparent tax contributions.
  - _Consumer Interests:_ Ensuring fair business, marketing, and advertising practices.
- **Unique Grievance Mechanism (NCPs):** A distinguishing feature of the OECD framework is the requirement for adhering governments to establish **National Contact Points (NCPs)**. NCPs are state-backed agencies tasked with promoting the guidelines and handling complaints when multinational corporations fail to meet the required standards. This provides a formal, non-judicial mechanism to resolve conflicts and hold corporations accountable for their CSR impacts.
