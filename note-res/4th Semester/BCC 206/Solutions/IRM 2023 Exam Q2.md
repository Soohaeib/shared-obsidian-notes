For each of the risks provided, an appropriate risk management strategy involves selecting techniques that either reduce the frequency and severity of the loss (**risk control**) or provide funding for the loss should it occur (**risk financing**).
The following are appropriate techniques and support for each scenario:

# (a)

#### Manufacturing company facing raw material price fluctuations

- **Technique:** **Hedging**.
- **Explanation:** Fluctuations in input prices constitute a **commodity price risk**, which is a type of speculative financial risk. Hedging is a technique for transferring the risk of unfavorable price fluctuations to a speculator by purchasing and selling futures contracts or other financial derivatives. By entering into a contract to buy raw materials at a predetermined price in the future, the company can lock in its costs and protect its profit margins.

# (b)

#### Commercial bank in Bangladesh facing credit risk in the agricultural sector

- **Technique:** **Risk Control (Loss Prevention)** and **Credit Insurance**.
- **Explanation:** Credit risk is the uncertainty that a borrower will fail to make promised payments. The bank can use **loss prevention** by implementing stricter underwriting and credit analysis to evaluate the creditworthiness of borrowers based on their capital, character, and capacity. Additionally, the bank can use **credit insurance** (or a credit guarantee), which compensates the lender if a borrower defaults on a loan due to commercial or political reasons.

# (c)

#### Negligent motorist ordered to pay a substantial liability judgment

- **Technique:** **Insurance (Liability Coverage)** and **Loss Prevention**.
- **Explanation:** This is a **liability risk**, which is particularly critical because there is no maximum upper limit on the potential loss. The most practical method for dealing with this risk is **liability insurance** (specifically Part A of a Personal Auto Policy), where the insurer agrees to pay damages for which the insured is legally responsible and provides a legal defense. **Loss prevention**, such as taking a safe-driving or defensive driving course, can also be used to reduce the probability of an accident occurring.

# (d)

#### Manufacturing company at risk of fire due to flammable materials

- **Technique:** **Risk Control (Loss Prevention and Reduction)** and **Commercial Property Insurance**.
- **Explanation:** Fire is a major **property risk**. **Loss prevention** measures, such as forbidding smoking in areas where flammable materials are stored, can reduce the frequency of fire. **Loss reduction** measures, such as installing an automatic sprinkler system, can reduce the severity of the damage after a fire starts. **Commercial Property Insurance** (such as a Building and Personal Property Coverage Form) provides risk transfer by indemnifying the firm for the direct physical damage to its assets.

# (e)

#### Agricultural exporter concerned about global price volatility

- **Technique:** **Hedging**.
- **Explanation:** Exporters face **market risk** and **commodity price risk** because global demand and supply conditions cause prices to be volatile. Similar to the manufacturer in scenario (a), the exporter can use **futures or options contracts** to lock in a guaranteed price for its products at the time of harvest or shipment. This transfers the speculative risk of a price decline to a speculator, ensuring the exporter receives a stable revenue regardless of market movements.
