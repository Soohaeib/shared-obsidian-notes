### (a)

#### Definitions of Entrepreneur, Corporate Entrepreneurship, and Entrepreneurship

##### Entrepreneur

An entrepreneur is an individual who recognizes an opportunity, mobilizes the necessary resources, and takes on the financial and personal risks to create, organize, and manage a new venture with the goal of generating economic value, innovation, or social impact. In economic theory, an entrepreneur acts as a catalyst for growth by introducing new products, optimization methods, or market approaches.

##### Corporate Entrepreneurship

Corporate entrepreneurship (also frequently referred to as intrapreneurship) is the conceptualization, development, and implementation of entrepreneurial strategies, mindsets, and behavioral practices within an established corporate organizational environment. It involves activities aimed at fostering internal innovation, creating new corporate ventures, or executing strategic renewal to sustain a firm's competitive advantage and respond to environmental disruptions.

##### Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is the dynamic and continuous process of identifying, evaluating, and exploiting market opportunities that have been previously unaddressed or underserved. It encompasses the creative destruction of existing market standard systems through the strategic configuration of risk management, resource orchestration, and innovative execution to deliver novel utility to a target marketplace.

### (b)

#### Qualities of a Successful Entrepreneur

##### Passion for the Business

Passion is the primary emotional and cognitive driver that sustains an entrepreneur through extended periods of uncertainty, institutional barriers, and financial strain. This characteristic enables individuals to remain focused on long-term execution and effectively communicate their organizational vision to initial stakeholders, capital partners, and employees.

##### Product and Customer Focus

Successful entrepreneurs maintain an unwavering strategic focus on the alignment between their product or service utility and the explicit or latent needs of their target customer base. This quality dictates that technological or process innovation must be driven by practical value creation rather than abstract engineering goals.

##### Tenacity Despite Failure

The developmental lifecycle of a startup involves frequent operational pivots, customer rejections, and commercial setbacks. Tenacity represents the psychological resilience required to treat individual failure as an iterative data loop for refining business structures, rather than a final endpoint for the enterprise.

##### Execution Intelligence

Execution intelligence is the capability to operationalize business concepts, plans, and abstract ideas into functional organizational workflows. It represents the transition from theoretical opportunity identification to tangible product delivery, resource allocation, and cash-flow management.

### (c)

#### Steps of the Entrepreneurial Process

##### Step 1: Deciding to Become an Entrepreneur

The process begins with an internal or situational trigger that prompts an individual to transition away from traditional wage employment or academic tracks. This choice is often motivated by an individual's desire for operational autonomy, financial independence, or the distinct determination to bring a proprietary solution to a glaring societal or market problem.

##### Step 2: Developing Successful Business Ideas

This step involves identifying concrete market opportunities, analyzing micro- and macro-environmental trends, and defining a viable value proposition. Entrepreneurs systematically perform several critical functions within this phase:

- **Opportunity Recognition:** Discerning patterns in consumer issues, regulatory shifts, or technological changes to locate strategic market gaps.
- **Feasibility Analysis:** Conducting preliminary product, industry, organizational, and financial evaluations to ensure the concept possesses structural viability before deploying capital.
- **Business Model Formulation:** Constructing a cohesive architectural plan that outlines how the firm will create, deliver, and capture economic value.
- **Business Plan Formulation:** Drafting a comprehensive document detailing operational strategies, financial projections, and target market matrices to secure external validation and alignment.

##### Step 3: Moving from an Idea to an Entrepreneurial Firm

Once the conceptual framework is validated, the entrepreneur transitions into structural assembly. This stage demands the formalization of legal protections, economic modeling, and organizational design:

- **Assembling the New-Venture Team:** Recruiting co-founders, key personnel, board advisors, and external legal or financial counsel to establish institutional competence.
- **Securing Initial Capital and Funding:** Identifying and sourcing appropriate capital structures (such as bootstrapping, personal lines of credit, angel investments, or venture capital funds) to finance initial inventory, prototype engineering, and early-stage runway requirements.

##### Step 4: Managing and Growing the Entrepreneurial Firm

The final phase focuses on stabilizing operations and scaling the business model within a competitive market ecosystem. Entrepreneurs must transition from speculative innovators to strategic corporate managers by implementing:

- **Operational Systems Control:** Implementing clear performance tracking metrics, financial controls, and human resource protocols.
- **Growth Strategies:** Executing deliberate internal expansions (such as new market penetration, product line extension, or digital scale) or external expansions (such as franchising, joint ventures, or strategic corporate acquisitions) while defending the business against the liability of organizational newness.
