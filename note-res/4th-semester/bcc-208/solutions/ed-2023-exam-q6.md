### (a)

#### Ethical Considerations in Entrepreneurship Development

Ethical considerations play a critical role in the sustainability, brand equity, and legal stability of any new entrepreneurial venture. Founders must proactively address ethical dynamics to build long-term trust with stakeholders and establish an untarnished market reputation. The core ethical considerations include:

- **Establishing a Strong Ethical Culture:** Entrepreneurs must consciously set an ethical "tone at the top." This involves leading by example, drafting a formal Code of Conduct, and implementing continuous ethics training for all incoming team members.
- **Fair Treatment of Stakeholders:** Maintaining complete transparency, honesty, and fairness with internal and external stakeholders, including employees, consumers, suppliers, and investors.
- **Respect for Intellectual Property (IP):** Ensuring the venture does not infringe upon the intellectual property rights of competitors (patents, trademarks, copyrights, or trade secrets) while ethically protecting its own proprietary innovations.
- **Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and Environmental Care:** Balancing the pursuit of profitability with a clear commitment to social and environmental sustainability. Ventures must minimize negative ecological footprints and contribute constructively to their local communities.
- **Honesty in Marketing and Financial Reporting:** Preventing deceptive advertising or misleading metrics. Financial records and disclosures presented to investors, lenders, and tax authorities must be accurate and objective.
- **Mitigating Conflicts of Interest:** Constructing unambiguous boundaries within corporate governance and founder agreements to prevent personal interests from undermining organizational integrity.

### (b)

#### Key Aspects of Standard and Disruptive Business Models

A business model represents a firm's plan or recipe for how it creates, delivers, and captures value for its stakeholders. Entrepreneurial firms generally utilize either standard or disruptive business models.

|**Aspect**|**Standard Business Models**|**Disruptive Business Models**|
|---|---|---|
|**Core Definition**|Depict existing plans or recipes that firms use to determine how they will create, deliver, and capture value within an established framework.|Highly impactful frameworks that do not fit standard profiles; they fundamentally change the way business is conducted across an entire industry or niche.|
|**Market Strategy**|They compete within traditional industry structures, optimizing existing value chains to capture market share from established rivals.|They introduce groundbreaking value propositions that either create entirely new markets or radically redefine existing ones.|
|**Innovation Focus**|Focus on incremental enhancements, operational efficiencies, and proven execution patterns.|Focus on structural, technological, or systemic breakthroughs that shift customer habits and expectations.|
|**Risk & Return Profile**|Lower systemic risk due to market predictability, but limited potential to capture dominant, industry-wide market share.|Exceptionally high risk due to unproven market mechanics, but offers exponential growth and industry leadership upon success.|
|**Examples**|Traditional franchise models, subscription-based meal kits, brick-and-mortar retail outlets, and direct-to-consumer manufacturing.|Peer-to-peer ride-sharing (e.g., Uber), digital streaming infrastructure (e.g., Netflix), and cloud service orchestration.|

### (c)

#### Elements of a New Venture Team

A new-venture team consists of the group of founders, key employees, and advisers that effectively transition a new venture from an initial idea into a fully functioning, operational enterprise. The primary elements include:

1. **Founders:** The core entrepreneurs who launch the firm. Their unique industry experience, educational backgrounds, and professional chemistry establish the foundational strength of the venture.
2. **Key Employees:** The earliest personnel hired to build operational capacity. They typically possess specialized skills (e.g., product development, specialized sales, operations) that directly complement the capabilities of the founding team.
3. **Board of Directors:** A panel of individuals legally elected by the corporation's shareholders to oversee management performance, fulfill fiduciary duties, and provide high-level strategic oversight.
4. **Board of Advisers:** A panel of industry experts asked by the firm’s managers to provide strategic counsel on a regular basis. Unlike a board of directors, an advisory board possesses no legal responsibility or fiduciary duty over the firm.
5. **Lenders and Investors:** Financial backers (such as angel investors, venture capitalists, or commercial bankers) who provide essential capital. They often contribute vital mentorship, critical network connections, and operational guidance.
6. **Other Professionals:** External service providers who offer critical corporate transaction, regulatory, and compliance support, such as startup attorneys, certified public accountants (CPAs), and strategic consultants.
