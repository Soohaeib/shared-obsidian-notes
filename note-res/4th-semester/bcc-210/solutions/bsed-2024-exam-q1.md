### (a)

#### Territories of Bangladesh

The territory of the People's Republic of Bangladesh is characterized by its strategic geographical location in South Asia, its evolving land boundaries, and its successfully resolved maritime boundaries.

- **Geographical Location and Land Area**: Bangladesh is situated between $20^\circ34'$ and $26^\circ38'$ north latitudes and $88^\circ01'$ and $92^\circ41'$ east longitudes. The total land area of the country is approximately 147,570 square kilometers, making it one of the most densely populated nations in the world.
- **Land Boundaries**: Bangladesh shares land borders with two countries:
  - **India**: Bordering the west, north, and northeast sides, the frontier spans approximately 4,156 kilometers.
  - **Myanmar**: Bordering the southeast side, this frontier extends for about 271 kilometers.
  - The southern coastline is bounded by the **Bay of Bengal**.
- **Enclave Exchange (Land Boundary Agreement)**: A historic feature of Bangladesh's land territory was the presence of complex enclaves (_Chhitmahals_) with India. This territorial anomaly was resolved permanently on the midnight of July 31, 2015, through the implementation of the landmark 1974 Land Boundary Agreement (LBA). Under this exchange, Bangladesh acquired 111 Indian enclaves (covering 17,160 acres), while India received 51 Bangladeshi enclaves (covering 7,110 acres), integrating thousands of citizens and streamlining the nation's sovereign map.
- **Maritime Territories**: Bangladesh successfully secured its sovereign rights over vast maritime zones through international legal arbitration:
  - In 2012, the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (ITLOS) settled the maritime boundary dispute with **Myanmar**.
  - In 2014, the Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA) settled the maritime boundary dispute with **India**.
  - Consequently, Bangladesh sustained its claim over a **12-nautical-mile territorial sea**, a **200-nautical-mile Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**, and sovereign rights over the continental shelf up to 354 nautical miles from the baseline. This added more than 118,813 square kilometers of smooth maritime territory to Bangladesh, opening up horizons for the "Blue Economy".

### (b)

#### Role of Language Movement in Emerging Bangladesh

The Language Movement (_Bhasha Andolon_) of 1948–1952 was the foundational milestone that served as the catalyst for the ultimate emergence of independent Bangladesh in 1971. It transformed a cultural-linguistic grievance into a political movement for self-determination.

- **Birth of Bengali Nationalism**: Prior to 1952, political identities in the region were heavily influenced by religious solidarity, which had led to the 1947 Partition of India. When the ruling elite of Pakistan declared that "Urdu and Urdu alone shall be the state language of Pakistan," it threatened the cultural existence of the 56% majority Bengali population. The resistance culminated on **February 21, 1952**, when students and citizens sacrificed their lives (including Salam, Barkat, Rafiq, Jabbar, and Shafiur). This sacrifice shattered the "Two-Nation Theory" and birthed an inclusive secular nationalism centered on linguistic identity.
- **Secularization of Politics**: The movement accelerated the transition of East Bengal's politics from communal orientations towards secular, progressive platforms. This ideological shift was evident when the _Awami Muslim League_ dropped the word "Muslim" from its name in 1955 to welcome non-Muslim populations into the mainstream political struggle.
- **Foundation for Future Democratic Struggle**: The Language Movement provided the structural blueprint for all subsequent mass movements against Pakistani military and bureaucratic hegemony. The organizational frameworks, student activism, and unity forged in 1952 directly fueled:
  - The United Front (_Juktofront_) electoral victory in 1954.
  - The anti-ayub education movements in 1962.
  - The historic **Six-Point Programme** of 1966 presented by Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.
  - The **Mass Upsurge of 1969** and the landslide election victory of 1970.
- **Inspiration During the Liberation War**: The spirit of _Ekushey February_ served as a deep psychological and emotional reservoir for freedom fighters during the 1971 Liberation War. The slogans, patriotic songs (such as _"Amar Bhaiyer Rokte Rangano Ekushey February"_), and literature born from the language movement inspired the nation to resist occupation and secure a sovereign state.

### (c)

#### Fall of the Juktofront Government

The United Front (_Juktofront_) coalition achieved a monumental landslide victory in the March 1954 East Bengal Provincial Assembly elections, completely dismantling the ruling Muslim League. However, the Juktofront cabinet, led by Sher-e-Bangla A.K. Fazlul Huq, was abruptly dismissed within just **56 days** of taking office. The premature fall of the government was driven by a combination of central conspiracies, industrial unrest, and internal coalition frictions.

- **Conspiracies of the Central Ruling Elite**: The West Pakistani civil-military bureaucratic oligarchy, spearheaded by Governor-General Malik Ghulam Muhammad, was deeply alarmed by the absolute democratic mandate achieved by Bengali leadership. They feared that a strong provincial government in East Bengal would successfully demand the regional autonomy outlined in the Juktofront's 21-point manifesto, thereby weakening central hegemony.
- **Instigation of Industrial Riots**: To create an artificial crisis of law and order, central intelligence agencies orchestrated and exacerbated violent labor riots between Bengali and non-Bengali workers at the **Adamjee Jute Mills** in Narayanganj and the **Karnafuli Paper Mills** in Chandraghona. Despite the provincial government's efforts to control the situation, the central government weaponized these tragic riots to brand the Juktofront administration as incapable of maintaining public order and protecting minorities.
- **Distortion of Fazlul Huq’s Statements**: During a visit to Calcutta (Kolkata) in May 1954, Chief Minister Sher-e-Bangla A.K. Fazlul Huq made emotional speeches emphasizing the historical, cultural, and linguistic commonalities between the two Bengals. The West Pakistani media and central authorities deliberately distorted his words, mischaracterizing him as a "traitor" who intended to split Pakistan and orchestrate the secession of East Bengal.
- **Internal Ideological Dissensions**: The Juktofront was not a single, homogeneous political party; it was an electoral alliance comprising disparate political factions, including the _Awami Muslim League_, _Krishak Sramik Party_, _Nizam-e-Islam_, and _Ganatantri Dal_. Once in power, internal rivalries regarding cabinet portfolios and political priorities emerged between leaders like A.K. Fazlul Huq and Huseyn Shaheed Suhrawardy, which weakened the coalition’s collective defense against external maneuvers.
- **Arbitrary Imposition of Section 92A**: Exploiting the engineered instability and manufactured media narrative, Governor-General Ghulam Muhammad officially dismissed the Juktofront ministry on **May 30, 1954**, under **Section 92A** of the Government of India Act 1935. Central rule (Governor's Rule) was imposed on East Bengal, and Defense Secretary Iskander Mirza was dispatched as Governor to unleash political repression, resulting in the immediate arrest of hundreds of leaders, including Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.
