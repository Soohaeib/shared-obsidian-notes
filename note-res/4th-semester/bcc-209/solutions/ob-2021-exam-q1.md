### (a)

#### As a student of Business Administration, why do you need to study Organizational Behavior?

For a student of Business Administration, studying Organizational Behavior (OB) is crucial because organizations are fundamentally composed of people. While technical disciplines like accounting, finance, and marketing provide the functional tools for business operations, OB provides the human insights necessary to manage and lead effectively.

- **Development of Interpersonal Skills:** Leadership in business administration requires more than just technical acumen. Understanding OB helps students develop essential "people skills" (such as communication, empathy, and conflict resolution), which are primary determinants of managerial success and career advancement.
- **Understanding and Predicting Human Behavior:** OB offers systematic frameworks to understand why individuals and groups act the way they do within a work environment. This predictive capacity allows future managers to anticipate employee reactions to organizational changes, policies, and structural shifts.
- **Enhancing Employee Motivation and Productivity:** By learning about motivational theories, job design, and reward systems, business administration students acquire the strategies needed to boost employee engagement, maximize productivity, and minimize costly organizational issues like absenteeism and turnover.
- **Navigating Diversity and Globalization:** Modern business environments are increasingly diverse and interconnected. OB trains students to manage workforce diversity, foster inclusive corporate cultures, and lead effectively across different global and cultural contexts.
- **Optimizing Organizational Design and Culture:** Business administrators must build structures that support strategic goals. OB provides insights into how organizational power, politics, culture, and design interact to shape overall company performance.

### (b)

#### What are the major behavioral science disciplines that contribute to OB?

Organizational Behavior is an applied behavioral science built upon contributions from several distinct behavioral and social disciplines. The major contributing disciplines include:

|**Discipline**|**Unit of Analysis**|**Primary Contributions to OB**|
|---|---|---|
|**Psychology**|Individual|Learning, motivation, personality, emotions, perception, training, leadership effectiveness, job satisfaction, individual decision-making, performance appraisal, attitude measurement, employee selection, work design, and work stress.|
|**Social Psychology**|Group|Behavioral change, attitude change, communication, group processes, group decision-making, power dynamics, and trust.|
|**Sociology**|Group / Organization System|Group dynamics, design of work teams, communication patterns, organizational power, conflict dynamics, intergroup behavior, formal organization theory, organizational technology, organizational change, and organizational culture.|
|**Anthropology**|Organization System|Comparative values, comparative attitudes, cross-cultural analysis, organizational culture, and distinct differences in organizational environments across international borders.|

### (c)

#### How do organizations manage diversity effectively?

Effective diversity management involves making everyone more aware of and sensitive to the needs and differences of others. To transform workforce diversity into a competitive advantage, organizations implement comprehensive, multi-level strategies:

- **Attracting, Selecting, Developing, and Retaining Diverse Employees:** Organizations can enhance diversity by targeting recruiting materials to underrepresented demographic groups, partnering with diverse professional associations, and ensuring that selection criteria are objective, standardized, and free from implicit bias.
- **Implementing Effective Diversity Training Programs:** Successful organizations utilize structured diversity training programs that usually focus on three distinct components:
  - Teaching managers about the legal framework for equal employment opportunity and encouraging fair treatment regardless of demographic characteristics.
  - Teaching managers how a diverse workforce is better equipped to serve a diverse market of customers and clients.
  - Fostering personal development practices that bring out the skills and abilities of all workers, acknowledging how varying perspectives can improve problem-solving.
- **Managing Diversity in Groups:** Managers can create high-functioning teams by focusing on common goals and establishing a culture of open communication. This helps convert demographic differences into informational advantages while mitigating potential faultlines or internal conflict.
- **Establishing Fair Institutional Policies:** Developing transparent career advancement tracks, formal mentoring programs for minority employees, and flexible work arrangements ensures that retention rates remain equitable across all employee demographics.
