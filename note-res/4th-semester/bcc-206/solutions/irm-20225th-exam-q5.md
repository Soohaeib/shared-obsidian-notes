### (a)

#### Comparison between Term Life and Whole Life Insurance

Term life insurance and whole life insurance are two fundamental types of life insurance products designed to meet different financial goals and risk management needs.

|**Feature**|**Term Life Insurance**|**Whole Life Insurance**|
|---|---|---|
|**Duration of Coverage**|Provides coverage for a specified period or "term" (e.g., 10, 15, 20, or 30 years).|Provides permanent, lifelong coverage as long as premiums are paid continuously.|
|**Cash Value Component**|No cash value accumulation. It is a pure insurance protection product.|Includes a savings/investment component known as cash value, which grows over time on a tax-deferred basis.|
|**Premium Structure**|Premiums are typically level during the chosen term but increase significantly if renewed after expiration.|Premiums are generally level and remain unchanged throughout the lifetime of the policy.|
|**Cost / Affordability**|Initial premiums are significantly lower and more affordable per unit of coverage.|Initial premiums are substantially higher to fund both the insurance protection and the cash value growth.|
|**Policy Maturity**|Expires without value at the end of the term if the insured survives.|Matures when the insured dies or reaches a specified advanced age (e.g., 100 or 121), at which point the cash value equals the face amount.|

#### Appropriate Scenarios and Examples

- **When Term Life Insurance is More Appropriate:**
  Term insurance is ideal for individuals seeking maximum protection at the lowest cost to cover temporary financial obligations.
  > **Example:** A 30-year-old individual who has recently taken out a 20-year home mortgage of Tk. 50,00,000 and has young children. If this individual purchases a 20-year term policy, it ensures that if they pass away during their peak income-earning and child-rearing years, the mortgage can be completely paid off and the children's education can be secured. Once the 20 years pass, the mortgage is paid, the kids are independent, and the need for large-scale insurance diminishes.
- **When Whole Life Insurance is More Appropriate:**
  Whole life insurance is suitable for individuals seeking lifetime financial protection coupled with a guaranteed savings mechanism, estate planning needs, or wealth preservation.
  > **Example:** A high-net-worth individual who wants to ensure there is immediate liquidity available to pay for estate taxes or final expenses upon their death, regardless of when it occurs, so that their family properties do not have to be liquidated under duress. Additionally, they can utilize the cash value component as a conservative alternative asset class that accumulates guaranteed growth over decades.

### (b)

#### Group Life Insurance vs. Individual Life Insurance

Group life insurance and individual life insurance differ primarily in terms of underwriting, administration, ownership, and contract flexibility.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages of Group Life Insurance

- **Advantages:**
  - **Lower Cost:** Because of mass administration and group risk sharing, the premium per person is typically much lower than an individual policy, often fully or partially subsidized by an employer.
  - **No Medical Underwriting:** For basic coverage limits, evidence of insurability is generally waived. This allows individuals with pre-existing medical conditions, who might otherwise be uninsurable, to obtain coverage.
  - **Convenience:** Enrollment is straightforward and requires minimal personal effort, with premiums often automatically deducted from payroll.
- **Disadvantages:**
  - **Lack of Portability:** The coverage is usually tied to employment. If the individual leaves the company, gets terminated, or retires, the policy typically lapses or becomes very expensive to convert into an individual plan.
  - **Limited Customization:** Policy terms, coverages, and choices are predetermined by the employer or master policyholder; the individual cannot add specialized riders or tailor the policy.
  - **Insufficient Limits:** The coverage amount is often determined as a fixed multiple of salary (e.g., 1x or 2x annual income), which may not satisfy the true total financial protection needs of a large family.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages of Individual Life Insurance

- **Advantages:**
  - **Complete Portability:** The policy is owned entirely by the individual. It remains active regardless of job changes, career moves, or retirement, as long as the premiums continue to be paid.
  - **High Customization:** The policyholder can select specific coverage amounts, choose policy durations, and append various optional riders such as accidental death benefits, critical illness cover, or waiver of premiums.
  - **Long-Term Security:** It allows for independent, personalized long-term financial planning without relying on third-party employer benefits.
- **Disadvantages:**
  - **Higher Cost:** Premiums are higher because the risk is evaluated on a single individual rather than distributed across an entire group, alongside higher administrative costs per policy.
  - **Strict Underwriting Requirements:** Applicants must undergo medical evaluations, disclose detailed health histories, and pass strict underwriting criteria. Individuals with severe health issues may face heavily rated premiums or absolute denial of coverage.

### (c)

#### Key Factors Influencing the Calculation of Life Insurance Premiums

The determination of life insurance premiums involves actuarial calculations built upon three primary structural components, modified by individual risk characteristics.

#### 1. Mortality Rates (The Primary Component)

Actuaries utilize mortality tables to determine the statistical probability of death occurring at any given age and gender.

- **Impact on the Individual:** As an individual ages, the probability of dying increases, which causes life insurance premiums to escalate dramatically for policies initiated later in life. Furthermore, because statistics show that women have a longer life expectancy than men on average, a female applicant will typically face lower life insurance premiums than a male applicant of the exact same age and health condition.

#### 2. Investment Earnings (Interest)

Life insurance companies do not keep premium revenue idle; they invest these funds in highly secure financial instruments (bonds, real estate, mortgages) to generate interest income.

- **Impact on the Individual:** Expected investment yields act as an offset to the ultimate cost of insurance. When general market interest rates are high and investment returns are strong, insurers can afford to charge lower net premiums because investment earnings will offset a larger portion of future claim obligations.

#### 3. Operating Expenses (Loading)

This factor accounts for the cost of running the insurance enterprise. It includes agent commissions, administrative overhead, medical examination costs, underwriting tools, premium taxes, and contingency reserves.

- **Impact on the Individual:** These costs are factored into the net premium as a "loading" fee to yield the final gross premium that the customer pays. An efficient insurer with lean operational costs can offer more competitive pricing to individual policyholders.

#### 4. Personal Risk Modifications (Health, Lifestyle, and Occupation)

Beyond base actuarial parameters, individual assessments tailor the ultimate price.

- **Impact on the Individual:**
  - _Health Status:_ Underlying conditions like hypertension, diabetes, or obesity lead to increased premium ratings.
  - _Lifestyle Choices:_ Habits like tobacco or alcohol usage significantly drive up mortality risk factors, resulting in steep surcharges.
  - _Occupation/Hobbies:_ Engaging in hazardous occupations (e.g., commercial aviation, mining) or dangerous hobbies (e.g., scuba diving) shifts an individual into higher risk classes, increasing overall premium costs.

### (d)

#### Quantitative Premium Calculation for Mr. Ahmed

To calculate the annual premium that Mr. Ahmed needs to pay, we analyze the specific pricing variables provided by the insurance provider:

- **Desired Coverage Amount (Face Value):** Tk. 35,00,000
- **Policy Term:** 15 years
- **Age of the Insured:** 40 years old
- **Applicable Age Tier:** Age 40–49
- **Base Premium Rate (for Age 40–49):** Tk. 0.75 per Tk. 1,000 of coverage
- **Smoker Surcharge Rate:** Tk. 2.00 per Tk. 1,000 of coverage

#### Step 1: Determine the Combined Premium Rate per Tk. 1,000 of Coverage

Because Mr. Ahmed is a smoker, both the base rate and the additional smoker surcharge apply to his calculation:
$\text{Total Rate per Tk. 1,000} = \text{Base Premium Rate} + \text{Smoker Surcharge}$
$\text{Total Rate per Tk. 1,000} = \text{Tk. } 0.75 + \text{Tk. } 2.00 = \text{Tk. } 2.75$

#### Step 2: Determine the Number of Thousands in Total Coverage

The premium rates are assigned per unit of Tk. 1,000. Therefore, we calculate how many units of Tk. 1,000 are contained within the overall coverage limit:
$\text{Units of Tk. 1,000} = \frac{\text{Total Coverage Amount}}{\text{Tk. } 1,000}$
$\text{Units of Tk. 1,000} = \frac{35,00,000}{1,000} = 3,500 \text{ units}$

#### Step 3: Compute the Final Annual Premium

Multiply the total units of coverage by the calculated total rate per unit:
$\text{Annual Premium} = \text{Units of Tk. 1,000} \times \text{Total Rate per Tk. 1,000}$
$\text{Annual Premium} = 3,500 \times 2.75 = \text{Tk. } 9,625$
**Conclusion:** Mr. Ahmed must pay a final total annual premium of **Tk. 9,625** for his desired term life policy.
