### (a)

#### Subject Matters of Marine Insurance

Marine insurance is a contract where the insurer undertakes to indemnify the insured against maritime losses, which are losses incident to marine adventure. The subject matter of marine insurance can be broadly classified into four main categories:

1. **Hull Insurance (The Ship)**
   - This relates to the insurance of the vessel itself, including its hull, machinery, furniture, equipment, boilers, and all tackle.
   - Hull insurance protects the shipowner against physical loss or damage to the ship caused by perils of the sea, fire, piracy, and collisions. It can be taken for a specific voyage or a specific period.
2. **Cargo Insurance**
   - This protects the owner of the goods or merchandise being transported by sea.
   - Cargo is exposed to numerous risks during transit, such as damage from seawater, fire, theft, rough handling, or total loss if the ship sinks. The coverage usually applies from the warehouse of origin to the warehouse of destination (Warehouse-to-Warehouse cover).
3. **Freight Insurance**
   - Freight is the fee or consideration paid to the shipowner for the safe transportation of goods.
   - According to maritime law, freight is typically payable only when the cargo safely reaches its destination. If the ship sinks or the cargo is lost mid-voyage, the shipowner loses the freight earnings. Freight insurance covers this specific financial loss.
4. **Liability Insurance (Protection and Indemnity - P\&I)**
   - This covers the legal liabilities incurred by the shipowner or operator to third parties due to maritime operations.
   - It includes liability for injury or loss of life of passengers/crew, damage caused to other ships due to collisions (Running Down Clause), damage to docks, piers, or wharves, and liabilities stemming from marine pollution (such as oil spills).

### (b)

#### Application of Legal Principles to Marine Insurance

The fundamental principles of insurance contracts apply uniquely and strictly to marine insurance due to the nature of international trade and maritime adventures.

##### Insurable Interest

- **Definition:** The insured must have a legally recognized financial relationship with the subject matter, meaning they benefit from its safety and suffer financial loss from its damage or destruction.
- **Application in Marine Insurance:** Unlike life or fire insurance where an insurable interest must exist at the inception of the policy, in marine insurance, the insurable interest **must exist at the time of the loss**.
- **Example:** A cargo buyer may purchase insurance before the goods are shipped. Even if they do not own the goods at the moment the policy is issued, they have an insurable interest if they own them at the time the ship encounters a loss at sea. Shipowners have an insurable interest in the hull and expected freight, while cargo owners have it in their goods.

##### Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei)

- **Definition:** Both parties to the insurance contract must display complete honesty and disclose all material facts accurately.
- **Application in Marine Insurance:** Since the underwriter (insurer) is often unable to physically inspect the ship or cargo located in a distant port, they rely entirely on the statements provided by the proposer. The insured is legally bound to disclose every material circumstance known, such as the age of the ship, exact nature of the cargo, specific route, and past loss history.
- **Impact of Breach:** If the insured misrepresents or conceals a material fact (e.g., hiding that the cargo is highly flammable), the contract becomes voidable at the option of the insurer, and any subsequent claim can be legally rejected.

##### Principle of Indemnity

- **Definition:** The insured can recover only the actual amount of financial loss sustained, aiming to restore them to the same financial position held prior to the loss. Profit-making is strictly prohibited.
- **Application in Marine Insurance:** Marine insurance operates primarily as a contract of indemnity, but with a practical modification known as **Valued Policies**.
- **The Valuation Exception:** Due to fluctuations in the market value of goods during long voyages, it is difficult to determine the exact value at the time of loss. Therefore, the insurer and insured agree upon a fixed value at the inception of the policy (typically including Cost + Freight + Insurance + a standard 10% profit margin). In the event of a total loss, the agreed-upon valuation is paid out, making it a modified form of indemnity.

### (c)

#### Various Clauses of Marine Insurance Policies

Marine insurance policies are highly standardized through specific clauses drafted by the **Institute of London Underwriters (ILU)**, globally recognized as the **Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC)**. These clauses define the scope of coverage, exclusions, and obligations.

|**Clause Type**|**Scope of Coverage**|**Core Characteristics**|
|---|---|---|
|**Institute Cargo Clauses (A)**|All Risks|This provides the widest coverage. It covers all risks of loss or damage to the subject matter except for explicitly stated exclusions (such as wear and tear, inherent vice, or war).|
|**Institute Cargo Clauses (B)**|Named Perils (Intermediate)|This is a restrictive policy covering specific risks like fire, explosion, stranding, sinking, capsizing, earthquake, lightning, washing overboard, and entry of sea/river water into the vessel.|
|**Institute Cargo Clauses (C)**|Basic Named Perils (Minimum)|This provides the cheapest and most limited coverage. It only covers major accidents like fire, explosion, stranding, grounding, sinking, capsizing, and collision liability.|

#### Specific Operational Clauses

- **War Clause**
  - Marine policies generally exclude losses from war, hostilities, or rebellion. To cover these risks, a separate Institute War Clause must be attached to the policy, ensuring coverage against damage caused by war mines, torpedoes, or hostile naval actions.
- **Strikes Clause**
  - This clause extends protection to losses or damage caused by strikers, locked-out workers, persons taking part in labor disturbances, riots, or civil commotions.
- **Sue and Labour Clause**
  - This clause places a legal duty on the insured (or their agents) to take all reasonable steps to safeguard, defend, and recover the insured property when a loss is imminent or has occurred. The insurer agrees to reimburse the insured for any expenses reasonably incurred during such efforts, over and above the regular policy limit.
- **Running Down Clause (RDC) / Collision Liability Clause**
  - Found in hull insurance, this clause protects the shipowner against third-party liability if their vessel collides with another ship. It covers the legal costs and damages payable to the owner of the other vessel for damage to its hull, cargo, or loss of life.
- **Free of Particular Average (FPA) Clause**
  - Under this clause, the insurer avoids liability for partial losses (particular average losses) unless the ship is stranded, sunk, or burnt. It protects insurers from minor cargo damage claims resulting from standard transit movements.
