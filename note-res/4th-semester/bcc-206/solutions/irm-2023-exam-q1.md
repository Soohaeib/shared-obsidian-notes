### (a)

#### Identification and Explanation of Economic Risks

#### (a\_I)

##### Terrorist Attack Alert

- **Risk Classification:** Fundamental Risk (or Systemic Risk) and Pure Risk.
- **Explanation:** A terrorist attack is classified as a fundamental risk because it is non-diversifiable and impacts a large segment of the population, society, or an entire nation simultaneously rather than a specific individual. It is also a pure risk because it presents only two possible outcomes: a loss (destruction of life, property, and economic disruption) or no loss (if the attack is successfully averted). There is no possibility of financial or economic gain.

#### (a\_II)

##### Total Disability in a Plant Explosion

- **Risk Classification:** Particular Risk and Pure Risk (Personal Risk).
- **Explanation:** This is a particular risk because the event (a plant explosion) and its immediate consequences affect a specific individual (the family head) and a limited group (the family or the factory), rather than the entire economy. It is a pure risk—specifically a personal risk—because the total disability results only in financial loss through the depletion of earning capacity and the accumulation of medical expenses. There is no potential for financial profit or gain from the injury.

#### (a\_III)

##### Investment in Corporate Stock (Grameenphone Shares)

- **Risk Classification:** Particular Risk and Speculative Risk (Financial Risk).
- **Explanation:** Purchasing shares of stock involves speculative risk because there are three possible outcomes: a financial gain (via capital appreciation or dividends), a loss (due to a decline in stock price), or breaking even. It is categorized as a particular risk because the financial consequences are borne entirely by the individual investor who chose to purchase those specific shares, without widely impacting the structural stability of the whole economy.

#### (a\_IV)

##### Interest Rate Fluctuations Affecting Mortgage Payments

- **Risk Classification:** Fundamental Risk and Speculative Risk (Financial Risk).
- **Explanation:** This scenario represents a fundamental risk because a change in interest rates by the central bank (Bangladesh Bank) acts as a macroeconomic force that simultaneously influences a large class of individuals (all variable-rate flat buyers and borrowers across the country). It is a speculative risk (specifically an interest rate risk) because macroeconomic rate adjustments create bidirectional uncertainty, potentially benefiting lenders or certain market segments while imposing losses or higher costs on borrowers.

### (b)

#### Types of Pure Risk Facing Individuals or Organisations

- **Personal Risk:**
  - **Description:** Risks that directly affect an individual's capability to earn income, maintain physical well-being, or safeguard assets.
  - **Example:** The risk of premature death of a family’s primary breadwinner, which cuts off the stream of future earnings needed to support dependents.
- **Property Risk:**
  - **Description:** The risk of facing financial losses due to direct damage, destruction, or theft of real or personal property.
  - **Example:** A commercial warehouse or manufacturing facility being completely destroyed by a structural fire, resulting in direct property replacement costs.
- **Liability Risk:**
  - **Description:** The risk of being held legally responsible for bodily injury or property damage inflicted upon a third party due to negligence or professional malpractice.
  - **Example:** A motorist negligently failing to stop at a traffic light, causing an accident that severely injures a pedestrian, resulting in court-ordered medical compensation.
- **Risk of Loss of Business Income (Indirect Property Loss):**
  - **Description:** The consequential risk where an individual or organization suffers indirect financial losses—such as lost profits or ongoing fixed expenses—following a direct physical damage event.
  - **Example:** A restaurant being forced to shut down operations for three months for repairs after a kitchen fire, causing a total loss of operating revenue while fixed rental expenses continue.

### (c)

#### Factors Driving the Increase in Risk Frequency and Severity Over Time

- **Technological Advances and Complexities:** The rapid evolution of technology introduces entirely new categories of risk that did not previously exist, such as large-scale cybersecurity breaches, systemic infrastructure failure, and automated operational errors. The highly integrated nature of modern information systems means a single technological vulnerability can lead to exponential, widespread economic losses.
- **Urbanisation and High Population Density:** Global demographic shifts have concentrated vast amounts of population and high-value physical infrastructure within localized urban clusters and coastal economic zones. Consequently, when a localized peril (such as an earthquake, flood, or industrial fire) occurs, the absolute magnitude and concentration of property values and human lives exposed to the hazard dramatically amplify the severity of the loss.
- **Climate Change and Environmental Degradation:** The systemic shifting of global climate patterns has directly increased both the frequency and severity of natural disasters, including severe floods, extreme cyclones, and prolonged droughts. For developing economies like Bangladesh, this results in recurring, high-intensity disasters that outpace traditional mitigation strategies.
- **Globalisation and Interconnected Supply Chains:** Modern production processes rely heavily on just-in-time logistics and international trade channels. A disruption in one part of the world—whether due to geopolitical conflict, health pandemics, or regulatory changes—creates cascading operational delays and financial losses across global dependencies, compounding business risk.
- **Evolving Legal and Regulatory Environments:** There is a progressive trend toward stricter liability laws, expanded consumer protection rights, and an increasing societal tendency to litigate. This expanding legal scope drastically escalates the frequency of liability claims and the severity of punitive and compensatory damages awarded against individuals and corporations.
