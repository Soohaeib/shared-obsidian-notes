Here’s a simple comparison table:

| Term | Main focus | What it tries to do | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Risk prevention** | Stop the event from happening | Reduce the chance of the hazard occurring | Fire inspections, cybersecurity training |
| **Loss prevention** | Stop or limit loss before it grows | Reduce the chance or severity of damage | Sprinklers, safety guards, alarms |
| **Loss reduction** | Limit damage after the event starts | Shrink the final loss | Emergency response, backups, containment |
| **Risk reduction** | Lower overall exposure to harm | Reduce likelihood, impact, or both | Training, controls, backups, planning |
| **Risk mitigation** | Broader umbrella term | Manage risk to an acceptable level | Insurance, controls, contingency plans |

## Quick intuition

- **Risk prevention** = “How do we stop it?”
- **Loss prevention** = “How do we keep damage from happening or getting worse?”
- **Loss reduction** = “How do we reduce damage once it’s underway?”
- **Risk reduction** = “How do we make the whole risk smaller?”
- **Risk mitigation** = “How do we manage the risk overall?”

## Important nuance

In real-world business and insurance language, these terms often overlap. People may use **risk reduction**, **loss prevention**, and **risk mitigation** in slightly different ways depending on the industry, but the table above is the cleanest way to separate them.
