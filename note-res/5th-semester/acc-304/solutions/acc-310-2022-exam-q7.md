### (a)
#### Bin Card and Stores Ledger
- **Bin Card**: A quantitative store record attached to or placed near the bin, shelf, or rack where materials are physically stored in the storeroom. It is maintained by the storekeeper to record physical quantities of materials received, issued, and remaining balance after every transaction. It contains no unit prices or monetary values.
- **Stores Ledger**: A central subsidiary ledger account kept in the cost accounting department. It maintains a detailed record for each material item, showing both physical quantities and monetary values (unit price and total monetary value) for receipts, issues, and ending balances.
- **Key Differences**:
    1. _Location_: Placed in the storeroom vs. maintained in the cost accounting office.
    2. _Maintained By_: Storekeeper vs. Cost accountant / ledger clerk.
    3. _Scope_: Physical quantities only vs. both quantities and monetary values.
    4. _Posting_: Updated immediately upon physical transaction vs. posted periodically from source documents like goods received notes and materials requisitions.
### (b)
#### Cost Centre and Cost Unit
- **Cost Centre**: A location, person, equipment item, or organizational subunit for which costs are accumulated and measured for control. In responsibility accounting, a cost center is a subunit whose manager is held accountable for costs only (and not revenues or investments). Examples include an assembly department, maintenance department, or a specific production line.
- **Cost Unit**: A standard unit of quantity of product, service, or time in relation to which costs are calculated, accumulated, or expressed. It serves as the primary base for determining average unit cost. Examples include per speaker system produced, per passenger-kilometer, per kilowatt-hour, or per direct labor-hour.
### (c)
#### Sunk Cost and Opportunity Cost
- **Sunk Cost**: A past (historical) cost that has already been incurred and cannot be changed or recovered by any present or future decision. Because sunk costs are past and unavoidable, they are completely irrelevant for future managerial decisions (e.g., book value of existing equipment or past R&D spending).
- **Opportunity Cost**: The contribution to operating income (or monetary benefit) that is forgone or rejected by choosing one course of action over its next-best alternative use of limited resources. Opportunity costs are relevant costs for decision-making when capacity or capital is constrained, even though they are not recorded in financial accounting accounting ledgers.
### (d)
#### Labour Turnover
- **Definition**: Labour turnover measures the rate of change in an organization's workforce over a specific period due to resignations, retirements, or dismissals relative to the average workforce size.
- **Measurement Methods**:
    1. _Separation Method_:

$$\text{Labour Turnover Rate} = \frac{\text{Number of Separations during the Period}}{\text{Average Number of Workers during the Period}} \times 100$$
2. _Replacement Method_:

$$\text{Labour Turnover Rate} = \frac{\text{Number of Workers Replaced during the Period}}{\text{Average Number of Workers during the Period}} \times 100$$
3. _Flux Method_:

$$\text{Labour Turnover Rate} = \frac{\text{Number of Separations} + \text{Number of Accessions}}{\text{Average Number of Workers during the Period}} \times 100$$
- **Impact**: High turnover increases cost through preventative costs (employee welfare, higher wages) and replacement costs (recruitment, selection, training, spoiled production, and lower productivity).
### (e)
#### Comprehensive Income Statement
- **Definition**: A primary financial statement (guided by IAS 1) that reports all non-owner changes in equity during an accounting period, providing a complete measure of total financial performance.
- **Components**:
    1. _Net Income (Profit or Loss)_: Operating and non-operating net revenues minus expenses incurred during the period.
    2. _Other Comprehensive Income (OCI)_: Items of income and expense (including reclassification adjustments) not recognized in net profit or loss as required by accounting standards (e.g., unrealized gains/losses on available-for-sale securities, revaluation surpluses on fixed assets, foreign currency translation gains/losses, and actuarial gains/losses on pension plans).
- **Presentation**: Can be presented either as a single continuous _Statement of Comprehensive Income_ or as two separate consecutive reports (an _Income Statement_ followed immediately by a _Statement of Comprehensive Income_).
