# --- Question 2(c) FIFO Method (Perpetual) ---
# Inventory state tracking: list of tuples (qty, rate)

inventory = [(800, 6.0)]
cogs_fifo = 0.0

transactions = [
    ('May 5', 'pur', 200, 7.0),
    ('May 9', 'pur', 200, 8.0),
    ('May 16', 'iss', 400, None),
    ('May 24', 'pur', 300, 9.0),
    ('May 27', 'iss', 500, None)
]

print("=== FIFO PERPETUAL ===")
for date, ttype, qty, rate in transactions:
    if ttype == 'pur':
        inventory.append((qty, rate))
        print(f"{date}: Purchased {qty} @ ${rate:.2f}. Inv: {inventory}")
    elif ttype == 'iss':
        rem_to_issue = qty
        issue_cost = 0.0
        new_inv = []
        for i_qty, i_rate in inventory:
            if rem_to_issue == 0:
                new_inv.append((i_qty, i_rate))
            elif i_qty <= rem_to_issue:
                issue_cost += i_qty * i_rate
                rem_to_issue -= i_qty
            else:
                issue_cost += rem_to_issue * i_rate
                new_inv.append((i_qty - rem_to_issue, i_rate))
                rem_to_issue = 0
        inventory = new_inv
        cogs_fifo += issue_cost
        print(f"{date}: Issued {qty} units, Cost = ${issue_cost:.2f}. Inv: {inventory}")

ending_inv_fifo = sum(q * r for q, r in inventory)
print(f"FIFO Cost of Materials Used: ${cogs_fifo:.2f}")
print(f"FIFO Ending Inventory (May 31): ${ending_inv_fifo:.2f}")

### (a)
#### Safety Stock Cost Analysis & Optimization
To determine the optimal safety stock level that minimizes the total annual cost of stockouts and inventory carrying costs, the expected stockout cost and safety stock carrying cost are calculated for each safety stock option.


$$
\text{Expected Annual Stockout Cost} = \text{Probability of Stockout} \times \text{Cost per Stockout Occurrence} \times \text{Number of Orders per Year}
$$



$$
\text{Safety Stock Carrying Cost} = \text{Safety Stock Level (Units)} \times \text{Annual Carrying Cost per Unit}
$$



$$
\text{Total Cost} = \text{Expected Annual Stockout Cost} + \text{Safety Stock Carrying Cost}
$$



$$
\begin{array}{rccrr} \hline \textbf{Safety Stock} & \textbf{Probability of} & \textbf{Expected Annual} & \textbf{Safety Stock} & \textbf{Total Annual} \\ \textbf{Level (Units)} & \textbf{Stockout} & \textbf{Stockout Cost (\$)} & \textbf{Carrying Cost (\$)} & \textbf{Cost (\$)} \\ \hline 0 & 40\% & 0.40 \times 30 \times 10 = 120.00 & 0 \times 0.50 = 0.00 & 120.00 \\ 50 & 20\% & 0.20 \times 30 \times 10 = 60.00 & 50 \times 0.50 = 25.00 & 85.00 \\ 100 & 10\% & 0.10 \times 30 \times 10 = 30.00 & 100 \times 0.50 = 50.00 & \mathbf{80.00} \\ 200 & 5\% & 0.05 \times 30 \times 10 = 15.00 & 200 \times 0.50 = 100.00 & 115.00 \\ \hline \end{array}
$$

##### Non-Financial and Qualitative Factors to Consider
1. **Supplier Dependability and Quality Assurance:** JIT production relies heavily on suppliers delivering 100% defect-free raw materials on time directly to the manufacturing line, as buffer inventories are minimized.
2. **Total Quality Management (TQM) Commitment:** Because safety stock is eliminated, any production defect or poor-quality batch will cause immediate line stoppage.
3. **Multifunctional and Flexible Workforce:** Workers must be cross-trained to perform setup changes, quality checks, and routine equipment maintenance dynamically.
4. **Equipment Reliability & Preventive Maintenance:** Machine breakdowns severely disrupt the demand-pull production schedule, making Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) imperative.
5. **Customer Lead-Time and Demand Predictability:** Production scheduling shifts from a traditional push system to a demand-driven pull system, requiring fast response times to customer orders.

#### Perpetual Inventory Costing Methods
#### (c_i)
##### First-In, First-Out (FIFO) Perpetual Method
Under the FIFO perpetual inventory method, costs are assigned to issues based on the earliest available purchase batches.


$$
\begin{array}{l | rrr | rrr | l} \hline \textbf{Date} & \multicolumn{3}{c|}{\textbf{Purchases}} & \multicolumn{3}{c|}{\textbf{Issues}} & \textbf{Balance} \\ & \text{Qty} & \text{Rate (\$)} & \text{Amt (\$)} & \text{Qty} & \text{Rate (\$)} & \text{Amt (\$)} & \text{Composition \& Value (\$)} \\ \hline \text{May 1} & - & - & - & - & - & - & 800 \text{ @ } \$6.00 = 4,800.00 \\ \text{May 5} & 200 & 7.00 & 1,400.00 & - & - & - & 800 \text{ @ } \$6.00; 200 \text{ @ } \$7.00 = 6,200.00 \\ \text{May 9} & 200 & 8.00 & 1,600.00 & - & - & - & 800 \text{ @ } \$6.00; 200 \text{ @ } \$7.00; 200 \text{ @ } \$8.00 = 7,800.00 \\ \text{May 16} & - & - & - & 400 & 6.00 & 2,400.00 & 400 \text{ @ } \$6.00; 200 \text{ @ } \$7.00; 200 \text{ @ } \$8.00 = 5,400.00 \\ \text{May 24} & 300 & 9.00 & 2,700.00 & - & - & - & 400 \text{ @ } \$6.00; 200 \text{ @ } \$7.00
\end{array}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: FIFO Method
> - **Cost of Materials Used (May Issues):** **$5,500.00**
> - **Cost Assigned to May 31 Inventory:** **$5,000.00**

> [!abstract] Working Notes - FIFO
> 1. **May 16 Issue (400 units):** Issued from May 1 beginning balance ($400 \times \$6.00 = \$2,400.00$).
> 2. **May 27 Issue (500 units):** Composed of remaining 400 units from May 1 balance ($400 \times \$6.00 = \$2,400.00$) plus 100 units from May 5 purchase ($100 \times \$7.00 = \$700.00$), giving a total issue cost of $\$3,100.00$. Total issues for May $= \$2,400.00 + \$3,100.00 = \$5,500.00$.
> 3. **May 31 Ending Inventory (600 units):** Composed of 100 units @ $\$7.00$ ($\$700.00$), 200 units @ $\$8.00$ ($\$1,600.00$), and 300 units @ $\$9.00$ ($\$2,700.00$), totaling $\$5,000.00$.

#### (c_ii)
##### Moving-Average (Perpetual Weighted Average) Method
Under the perpetual inventory system, the weighted-average method calculates a new moving average unit cost after every purchase transaction.


$$
\begin{array}{l | rrr | rrr | rrr} \hline \textbf{Date} & \multicolumn{3}{c|}{\textbf{Purchases}} & \multicolumn{3}{c|}{\textbf{Issues}} & \multicolumn{3}{c}{\textbf{Balance}} \\ & \text{Qty} & \text{Rate (\$)} & \text{Amt (\$)} & \text{Qty} & \text{Rate (\$)} & \text{Amt (\$)} & \text{Qty} & \text{Rate (\$)} & \text{Amt (\$)} \\ \hline \text{May 1} & - & - & - & - & - & - & 800 & 6.0000 & 4,800.00 \\ \text{May 5} & 200 & 7.00 & 1,400.00 & - & - & - & 1,000 & 6.2000 & 6,200.00 \\ \text{May 9} & 200 & 8.00 & 1,600.00 & - & - & - & 1,200 & 6.5000 & 7,800.00 \\ \text{May 16} & - & - & - & 400 & 6.5000 & 2,600.00 & 800 & 6.5000 & 5,200.00 \\ \text{May 24} & 300 & 9.00 & 2,700.00 & - & - & - & 1,100 & 7.1818 & 7,900.00 \\ \text{May 27} & - & - & - & 500 & 7.1818 & 3,590.91 & 600 & 7.1818 & \underline{\underline{4,309.09}} \\ \hline \textbf{Total} & & & & \mathbf{900} & & \mathbf{\underline{\underline{6,190.91}}} & & & \\ \hline \end{array}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: Moving Average Method
> - **Cost of Materials Used (May Issues):** **$6,190.91**
> - **Cost Assigned to May 31 Inventory:** **$4,309.09**

> [!abstract] Working Notes - Moving Average
> 1. **May 5 Moving Average Rate:** $\frac{\$4,800.00 + \$1,400.00}{800 + 200} = \frac{\$6,200.00}{1,000} = \$6.2000/\text{unit}$.
> 2. **May 9 Moving Average Rate:** $\frac{\$6,200.00 + \$1,600.00}{1,000 + 200} = \frac{\$7,800.00}{1,200} = \$6.5000/\text{unit}$.
> 3. **May 16 Issue Cost:** $400 \text{ units} \times \$6.5000 = \$2,600.00$. Remaining Balance $= \$5,200.00$ (800 units @ $\$6.5000$).
> 4. **May 24 Moving Average Rate:** $\frac{\$5,200.00 + \$2,700.00}{800 + 300} = \frac{\$7,900.00}{1,100} = \$7.181818/\text{unit}$.
> 5. **May 27 Issue Cost:** $500 \text{ units} \times \$7.181818 = \$3,590.91$. Total Cost of Materials Used $= \$2,600.00 + \$3,590.91 = \$6,190.91$.
> 6. **May 31 Ending Inventory Cost:** $600 \text{ units} \times \$7.181818 = \$4,309.09$.
> 7. _Periodic Weighted Average Alternative Note:_ If evaluated periodically across the entire month, the average unit rate is $\frac{\$10,500}{1,500} = \$7.00/\text{unit}$, yielding COGS of $\$6,300.00$ ($900 \times \$7.00$) and Ending Inventory of $\$4,200.00$ ($600 \times \$7.00$).
