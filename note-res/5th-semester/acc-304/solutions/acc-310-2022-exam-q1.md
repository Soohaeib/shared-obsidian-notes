### (a)
#### Definition of Financial Accounting and Cost Accounting, and Their Relationships
**1. Definition of Financial Accounting:** Financial accounting is the branch of accounting concerned with the summary, analysis, and reporting of financial transactions related to a business. It involves the preparation of financial statements (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement) for external users—such as shareholders, creditors, financial institutions, tax authorities, and regulators—in accordance with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) or International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS).

**2. Definition of Cost Accounting:** Cost accounting is a specialized branch of accounting that records, classifies, allocates, analyzes, and controls the costs of manufacturing products or offering services. It provides detailed quantitative and cost information to internal management for cost ascertainment, cost control, inventory valuation, profitability analysis, and operational decision-making.

**3. Relationship Among Financial Accounting, Cost Accounting, and Management Accounting:**
- **Financial Accounting vs. Cost Accounting:**
    - Financial accounting provides the overall historical financial performance and financial position of an organization as a whole to external stakeholders. Cost accounting provides detailed cost data per unit, department, or job to assist internal managers in ascertaining product costs and controlling expenditures.
    - Cost accounting feeds financial accounting with necessary inventory valuation data (Raw Materials, Work-in-Process, Finished Goods) and Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) required for financial statement preparation.
- **Cost Accounting vs. Management Accounting:**
    - Cost accounting focuses primarily on cost determination, cost accumulation, and cost control techniques.
    - Management accounting is broader in scope; it integrates cost accounting data, financial accounting data, and non-financial operational information to formulate business strategies, conduct long-term planning, make capital budgeting decisions, and evaluate organizational performance.
    - Cost accounting acts as the fundamental quantitative engine and foundational tool for management accounting.
- **Interrelationship (Synergy):**
    - Financial accounting, cost accounting, and management accounting form an integrated information system within an enterprise.
    - Financial accounting records raw financial transactions; cost accounting extracts cost operational details to determine cost of production and identify inefficiencies; management accounting synthesizes both financial and cost metrics to make forward-looking tactical and strategic decisions.
### (b)
#### Methods of Costing and Suitable Enterprises
A **method of costing** refers to the system applied to ascertain and accumulate the cost of a product or service based on the nature of manufacturing operations, production flow, and industry characteristics.
1. **Job Costing:**
    - **Concept:** Applied when work is undertaken based on customer's specific orders or distinct job specifications. Each job is treated as a separate cost object, and costs (direct materials, direct labor, overheads) are accumulated individually on job cost sheets.
    - **Suitable Enterprise:** Printing presses, custom furniture manufacturers, automobile repair workshops, and advertising agencies.
2. **Contract Costing:**
    - **Concept:** A specialized form of job costing applied to large-scale, long-term non-repetitive construction or engineering projects that typically extend over more than one accounting period.
    - **Suitable Enterprise:** Civil engineering construction firms (building bridges, highways, dams) and shipbuilding yards.
3. **Process Costing:**
    - **Concept:** Applied where production is continuous, standardized, and mass-produced through a continuous sequence of processes or operations. Production costs are accumulated by department or process for a period and averaged over total units produced.
    - **Suitable Enterprise:** Chemical processing plants, oil refineries, textile mills, cement manufacturing companies, and paper mills.
4. **Batch Costing:**
    - **Concept:** A variation of job costing where products are manufactured in discrete batches of identical items. Cost per unit is derived by dividing total batch manufacturing cost by the number of units produced in the batch.
    - **Suitable Enterprise:** Pharmaceutical companies (drug production runs), footwear manufacturers, component parts manufacturing, and commercial bakeries.
5. **Service / Operating Costing:**
    - **Concept:** Applied by undertakings that render services rather than manufacturing tangible physical goods. Costs are accumulated and calculated per composite unit of service (e.g., passenger-kilometer, bed-day).
    - **Suitable Enterprise:** Transport undertakings (bus operators, airlines), hospitals, hotels, electricity supply companies, and educational institutions.
6. **Single / Output / Unit Costing:**
    - **Concept:** Employed where production is uniform, continuous, and consists of a single product or a few grades of similar products. Cost is calculated per unit of output.
    - **Suitable Enterprise:** Brickworks, coal mining, flour mills, and stone quarries.
7. **Multiple / Composite Costing:**
    - **Concept:** Used in complex manufacturing industries where a final product is assembled from various manufactured components, requiring a combination of different costing methods.
    - **Suitable Enterprise:** Automobile manufacturing plants, aircraft manufacturers, and consumer electronics assembly plants.
### (c)
#### Cost Accounting Techniques for Cost Ascertainment and Control
#### (c_i)
##### Absorption Costing
- **Ascertainment:** Under absorption costing (full costing), both fixed and variable manufacturing overheads are absorbed into product cost. Direct materials, direct labor, variable factory overhead, and allocated fixed factory overhead are included in unit product costs and inventory valuation.
- **Control:** Cost control is exercised by setting predetermined overhead absorption rates based on budgeted capacity, followed by variance analysis of under-absorbed or over-absorbed factory overhead at period end.
#### (c_ii)
##### Marginal Costing
- **Ascertainment:** Under marginal costing (variable costing), only variable manufacturing costs (direct materials, direct labor, variable overhead) are charged to product cost and inventory. Fixed manufacturing overheads are treated as period costs and charged directly to the profit and loss account.
- **Control:** Facilitates cost control through Contribution ($\text{Contribution} = \text{Sales} - \text{Variable Costs}$) and Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) analysis. It assists management in short-term decision-making (e.g., make or buy, special order pricing, product mix) without distortion from fixed overhead allocations.
#### (c_iii)
##### Life Cycle Costing
- **Ascertainment:** Tracks and accumulates all revenues and costs attributable to a product across its entire life cycle—from initial research & development, design, and testing through manufacturing, marketing, distribution, customer service, and eventual disposal/decommissioning.
- **Control:** Enables cost control during the design and development phase where the vast majority of total life-cycle costs are committed or locked in, preventing costly engineering modifications and high post-launch maintenance expenses.
#### (c_iv)
##### Target Costing
- **Ascertainment:** Begins with a market-driven target selling price and subtracts the desired profit margin to determine the allowable target cost ($\text{Target Cost} = \text{Target Selling Price} - \text{Target Profit Margin}$).
- **Control:** Enforces proactive cost reduction before production begins. If estimated costs exceed target costs, multidisciplinary teams apply Value Engineering (VE) and design optimization to eliminate non-value-added activities and reduce manufacturing costs to the target level.
#### (c_v)
##### Standard Costing
- **Ascertainment:** Establishes predetermined standard costs for direct materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead per unit of output under expected efficient operating conditions.
- **Control:** Controls costs by systematically comparing actual incurred costs with pre-established standard benchmarks to calculate variances (material price/quantity variances, labor rate/efficiency variances, overhead variances). Unfavorable variances alert management to operational inefficiencies under "Management by Exception".
#### (c_vi)
##### Budgeting
- **Ascertainment:** Quantifies planned business operations into comprehensive financial budgets (sales, production, material usage, labor, overhead, cash budgets) for a specified future period.
- **Control:** Serves as a vital control mechanism through budgetary control systems. Actual financial performance is continuously compared against budgeted targets, allowing responsibility centers to identify variances and take timely corrective actions.
