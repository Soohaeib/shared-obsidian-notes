### (a)
#### Distinguish between Normal Costing and Actual Costing and the Role of Predetermined Overhead Rate
**1. Distinguish between Normal Costing and Actual Costing:**
- **Actual Costing System:**
    - **Direct Costs:** Direct materials and direct manufacturing labor are traced to a cost object by using the actual direct-cost rates multiplied by the actual quantities of the direct-cost inputs used.
    - **Indirect Costs:** Manufacturing overhead (MOH) is allocated to a cost object by using the **actual overhead rate** multiplied by the actual quantities of the cost-allocation bases used.
    - **Timing:** Actual overhead rates can only be calculated at the end of the accounting period after total actual indirect manufacturing costs and total actual allocation base quantities are fully known.
- **Normal Costing System:**
    - **Direct Costs:** Direct materials and direct manufacturing labor are traced to a cost object by using actual direct-cost rates multiplied by actual quantities of direct-cost inputs used (identical to actual costing).
    - **Indirect Costs:** Manufacturing overhead is allocated to a cost object by using a **predetermined overhead rate (POHR)** calculated at the beginning of the period, multiplied by actual quantities of the cost-allocation bases used.
    - **Timing:** Indirect costs are assigned continuously as production takes place throughout the operating period.

|**Cost Element**|**Actual Costing**|**Normal Costing**|
|---|---|---|
|**Direct Materials**|Actual Input Quantity $\times$ Actual Direct Rate|Actual Input Quantity $\times$ Actual Direct Rate|
|**Direct Labor**|Actual Input Quantity $\times$ Actual Direct Rate|Actual Input Quantity $\times$ Actual Direct Rate|
|**Manufacturing Overhead**|Actual Allocation Base $\times$ **Actual Overhead Rate**|Actual Allocation Base $\times$ **Predetermined Overhead Rate**|

**2. Essential Role of Predetermined Overhead Rates for Timely Decision-Making:**
- **Immediate Job Costing and Pricing:** Waiting until year-end to compute actual overhead rates prevents management from knowing total job costs during the period. A predetermined overhead rate allows management to calculate job costs immediately upon completion to determine prices, submit job quotes, and record sales margins.
- **Smoothing Seasonal and Volatile Overhead Fluctuations:** Actual overhead costs fluctuate widely due to seasonal utility bills, annual insurance premiums, or periodic maintenance, while monthly production volume varies. An actual costing system yields fluctuating unit costs across months. A predetermined rate averages annual overhead over annual activity, providing normalized unit costs.
- **Operational Control and Performance Evaluation:** Predetermined rates establish a baseline for evaluating performance. Management can analyze underapplied or overapplied overhead variances periodically to detect operational inefficiencies and take timely corrective actions.

#### Statement of Cost and Profit Computation for Peterson Manufacturing Ltd.

$$
\begin{array}{lrr} \hline \textbf{Particulars} & \textbf{Amount (Tk.)} & \textbf{Amount (Tk.)} \\ \hline \text{Direct Materials Used:} & & \\ \quad \text{Stock of Direct Materials (1-1-2024)} & 21,000 & \\ \quad \text{Add: Purchases of Direct Materials} & 74,000 & \\ \hline \quad \text{Direct Materials Available for Use} & 95,000 & \\ \quad \text{Less: Stock of Direct Materials (31-12-2024)} & (23,000) & \\ \hline \textbf{Cost of Direct Materials Used} & & \mathbf{72,000} \\ \text{Direct Manufacturing Labor} & & 22,000 \\ \text{Direct Expenses (Specialized tool hire)} & & 5,000 \\ \hline \textbf{Prime Cost} & & \mathbf{99,000} \\ \text{Manufacturing Overhead Costs:} & & \\ \quad \text{Indirect Manufacturing Labor} & 17,000 & \\ \quad \text{Plant Insurance} & 7,000 & \\ \quad \text{Depreciation—Plant, Building, and Equipment}
\end{array}
$$

#### Step-by-Step Calculations
1. **Cost of Direct Materials Used:**


$$
\text{Cost of Direct Materials Used} = \text{Opening Direct Materials} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Ending Direct Materials}
$$



$$
\text{Cost of Direct Materials Used} = 21,000 + 74,000 - 23,000 = \text{Tk. } 72,000 \quad \text{}
$$

2. **Prime Cost:**


$$
\text{Prime Cost} = \text{Direct Materials Used} + \text{Direct Manufacturing Labor} + \text{Direct Expenses}
$$



$$
\text{Prime Cost} = 72,000 + 22,000 + 5,000 = \text{Tk. } 99,000 \quad \text{}
$$

3. **Manufacturing Overhead Costs:**


$$
\text{Gross MOH} = \text{Indirect Labor} + \text{Plant Insurance} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Repairs \& Maintenance}
$$



$$
\text{Gross MOH} = 17,000 + 7,000 + 11,000 + 3,000 = \text{Tk. } 38,000 \quad \text{}
$$



$$
\text{Net MOH} = \text{Gross MOH} - \text{Sales of Scrap} = 38,000 - 2,000 = \text{Tk. } 36,000 \quad \text{}
$$

4. **Cost of Goods Manufactured (COGM):**


$$
\text{Total Manufacturing Cost} = \text{Prime Cost} + \text{Net MOH} = 99,000 + 36,000 = \text{Tk. } 135,000 \quad \text{}
$$



$$
\text{COGM} = \text{Total Manufacturing Cost} + \text{Opening WIP} - \text{Ending WIP}
$$



$$
\text{COGM} = 135,000 + 26,000 - 25,000 = \text{Tk. } 136,000 \quad \text{}
$$

5. **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):**


$$
\text{COGS} = \text{COGM} + \text{Opening Finished Goods} - \text{Ending Finished Goods}
$$



$$
\text{COGS} = 136,000 + 13,000 - 20,000 = \text{Tk. } 129,000 \quad \text{}
$$

6. **Cost of Sales:**


$$
\text{Cost of Sales} = \text{COGS} + \text{Marketing Costs} + \text{Administrative Costs}
$$



$$
\text{Cost of Sales} = 129,000 + 91,000 + 24,000 = \text{Tk. } 244,000 \quad \text{}
$$

7. **Total Sales Value:**


$$
\text{Profit} = 25\% \text{ of Sales} \implies \text{Cost of Sales} = 75\% \text{ of Sales}
$$



$$
\text{Total Sales Value} = \frac{\text{Cost of Sales}}{1 - 0.25} = \frac{244,000}{0.75} = \text{Tk. } 325,333.33 \quad \text{}
$$


> [!abstract] Working Notes
> - **Direct Expenses:** Specialized tool hire (Tk. 5,000) is directly identifiable with job/production orders and is therefore classified as a direct expense included under Prime Cost.
> - **Scrap Value Treatment:** Sales of scrap from production (Tk. 2,000) represents a recovery of indirect production costs and is deducted from Gross Manufacturing Overhead to arrive at Net Manufacturing Overhead.
> - **Profit Calculation:** Given that profit is $25\%$ on Sales, the Cost of Sales represents $100\% - 25\% = 75\%$ of Total Sales Value. Profit amount $= 325,333.33 \times 25\% = \text{Tk. } 81,333.33$.
