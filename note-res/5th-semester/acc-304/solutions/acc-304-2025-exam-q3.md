### (ii)
#### Computation of Underallocated or Overallocated Manufacturing Overhead
To determine the net overhead variance, actual manufacturing overhead incurred is compared against allocated manufacturing overhead applied to production.

$$
\text{Manufacturing Overhead Variance} = \text{Actual MOH Incurred} - \text{Allocated MOH Applied}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{lrr} \hline \textbf{Manufacturing Overhead Component} & \textbf{Amount (\$)} \\ \hline \text{Indirect materials issued} & \$25,000 \\ \text{Indirect manufacturing labor incurred} & \$80,000 \\ \text{Depreciation on plant and equipment} & \$65,000 \\ \text{Miscellaneous manufacturing overhead} & \$40,000 \\ \hline \textbf{Total Actual Manufacturing Overhead Incurred} & \mathbf{\$210,000} \\ \text{Less: Allocated Manufacturing Overhead (4,000 MH } \times \$50/\text{MH)} & (\$200,000)
$$

Because actual manufacturing overhead incurred ($210,000) exceeds allocated manufacturing overhead ($200,000), manufacturing overhead is **underallocated** by **$10,000**.

> [!success] Final Answer: Underallocated Manufacturing Overhead = $10,000

### (iii)
#### Disposal of Overhead Difference
#### Write-off Directly to Cost of Goods Sold
##### Write-Off Directly to Cost of Goods Sold
Under the direct write-off approach, the entire $10,000 underallocated manufacturing overhead variance is debited directly to Cost of Goods Sold, closing out both temporary manufacturing overhead accounts.

**Year-End Closing Journal Entry:**

$$
\begin{array}{llrr} \hline \textbf{Account Title \& Description} & \textbf{Debit (\$)} & \textbf{Credit (\$)} \\ \hline \text{Manufacturing Overhead Allocated} & \$200,000 & \\ \text{Cost of Goods Sold} & \$10,000 & \\ \quad \text{Manufacturing Overhead Control} & & \$210,000 \\ \text{(To close MOH accounts and write off underallocated overhead to COGS)} & & \\ \hline \hline \end{array}
$$

**Adjusted Balance Computation:**

$$
\text{Adjusted COGS} = \text{Unadjusted COGS} + \text{Underallocated MOH}
$$

$$
\text{Adjusted COGS} = \$750,000 + \$10,000 = \$760,000
$$

> [!success] Final Answer: Adjusted Cost of Goods Sold = $760,000

#### Proration Based on Overhead Allocated in Ending Balances
##### Proration Based on Overhead Allocated in Ending Balances
Under this method, the $10,000 underallocated overhead is prorated among Work-in-Process Control, Finished Goods Control, and Cost of Goods Sold based on the proportion of current-year allocated manufacturing overhead contained in each account's pre-proration ending balance.

**Proration Schedule:**

$$
\begin{array}{lrrrrr} \hline \textbf{Account} & \textbf{Ending Balance} & \textbf{Allocated MOH} & \textbf{\% of Total MOH} & \textbf{Proration Share} & \textbf{Adjusted Balance} \\ \hline \text{Work-in-Process Control} & \$50,000 & \$10,000 & 5.00\% & \$500 & \$50,500 \\ \text{Finished Goods Control} & \$100,000 & \$30,000 & 15.00\% & \$1,500 & \$101,500 \\ \text{Cost of Goods Sold} & \$750,000 & \$160,000 & 80.00\% & \$8,000 & \$758,000 \\ \hline \textbf{Total} & \mathbf{\$900,000} & \mathbf{\$200,000} & \mathbf{100.00\%} & \mathbf{\$10,000} & \mathbf{\$910,000} \\ \hline \hline \end{array}
$$

> [!abstract] Working Notes
> - WIP Overhead Proportion: $\frac{\$10,000}{\$200,000} = 5.00\% \implies 5.00\% \times \$10,000 = \$500$.
> - Finished Goods Overhead Proportion: $\frac{\$30,000}{\$200,000} = 15.00\% \implies 15.00\% \times \$10,000 = \$1,500$.
> - COGS Overhead Proportion: $\frac{\$160,000}{\$200,000} = 80.00\% \implies 80.00\% \times \$10,000 = \$8,000$.

> [!success] Final Answer: Prorated Additions: WIP Control = $500, Finished Goods Control = $1,500, Cost of Goods Sold = $8,000. Post-proration balances: WIP Control = $50,500, Finished Goods Control = $101,500, Cost of Goods Sold = $758,000.

#### Evaluation of Disposal Methods
#### Evaluation of Disposal Methods for Balance Sheet Accuracy
- **Superior Accuracy of Proration:** Proration based on the amount of overhead already allocated in ending balances (Method b) provides a significantly more accurate representation of the balance sheet at year-end than writing off the entire variance to Cost of Goods Sold.
- **Restatement to Actual Costs:** Proration restates ending inventory asset balances (Work-in-Process Control and Finished Goods Control) to what they would have been if actual manufacturing overhead cost rates had been used throughout the period rather than budgeted rates.
- **Prevention of Asset Understatement:** Writing off the entire $10,000 underallocated overhead exclusively to Cost of Goods Sold expenses all overhead variance immediately in the current period. This leaves ending Work-in-Process ($50,000) and Finished Goods ($100,000) inventories understated on the year-end balance sheet, distorting current asset totals and inventory valuation.
- **Conformity with GAAP & Conceptual Alignment:** Because the proration is calculated directly on the current period's overhead remaining in each account, it allocates the variance in exact proportion to where the manufacturing overhead costs actually reside. This strictly aligns with the historical cost principle and GAAP inventory reporting standards.
