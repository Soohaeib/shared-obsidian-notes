### (a)
#### Economic Order Quantity and Factors Considered in Determination
Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) is an inventory management technique that calculates the optimal purchase order size for a product to minimize total annual inventory costs. Total relevant inventory costs consist of annual ordering costs and annual inventory carrying (holding) costs. EOQ represents the balance point where annual ordering costs equal annual inventory carrying costs.

The mathematical formula for Economic Order Quantity is expressed as:


$$
\text{EOQ} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times U \times O}{S}}
$$


Where:
- $U = \text{Annual usage or demand of material in units}$
- $O = \text{Cost of placing an order}$
- $S = \text{Annual carrying cost per unit per year } (S = \text{Purchase Price per Unit} \times \text{Carrying Cost Percentage})$
##### Factors to be Considered in Determining Economic Order Quantity
1. **Annual Usage / Requirement ($U$):** The total volume of material or components consumed by the business during an accounting period. Higher annual consumption increases the optimal batch size.
2. **Ordering Cost per Order ($O$):** Fixed expenses incurred every time a purchase order is created and processed, including requisition preparation, purchase order issuance, shipping and handling, inspection, and administrative expenses. Higher ordering costs incentivize larger purchase quantities to minimize order frequency.
3. **Inventory Carrying / Holding Cost ($S$):** Expenses associated with holding inventory in storehouses over time, including opportunity cost of capital tied up, warehouse rent, insurance premiums, taxes, material handling, deterioration, theft, and obsolescence. Higher carrying costs incentivize smaller order sizes to minimize stock levels.
4. **Unit Purchase Price ($C$):** The acquisition cost per unit of inventory. When carrying costs are expressed as a percentage of unit cost, changes in purchase price directly alter the unit storage cost.
