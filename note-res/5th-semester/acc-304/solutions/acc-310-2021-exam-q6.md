### (a)
#### Job Costing and Price Calculation
##### 1. Predetermined Overhead Rates Computation
The predetermined overhead rates (POHR) for variable overheads are calculated separately for each department based on labor hours, while the fixed overhead rate is calculated on a plantwide basis over normal working hours.
- **Variable Overhead Rate per Direct Labor Hour (DLH)**:

$$\text{POHR}_{\text{Var}} = \frac{\text{Estimated Departmental Variable Overhead Cost}}{\text{Estimated Departmental Direct Labor Hours}}$$
- **Department A**:

$$\text{POHR}_{\text{Var, Dept A}} = \frac{\$5,000}{5,000 \text{ DLH}} = \$1.00 \text{ per DLH} \quad \text{}$$
- **Department B**:

$$\text{POHR}_{\text{Var, Dept B}} = \frac{\$3,000}{1,500 \text{ DLH}} = \$2.00 \text{ per DLH} \quad \text{}$$
- **Department C**:

$$\text{POHR}_{\text{Var, Dept C}} = \frac{\$2,000}{500 \text{ DLH}} = \$4.00 \text{ per DLH} \quad \text{}$$
- **Fixed Overhead Rate per Direct Labor Hour (DLH)**:

$$\text{POHR}_{\text{Fixed}} = \frac{\text{Estimated Total Fixed Overhead Cost}}{\text{Estimated Normal Working Hours}}$$

$$\text{POHR}_{\text{Fixed}} = \frac{\$20,000}{10,000 \text{ DLH}} = \$2.00 \text{ per DLH} \quad \text{}$$
##### 2. Direct Costs and Overhead Allocation for Job No. 2390
- **Direct Wages Incurred**:
    - Department A: $60 \text{ hrs} \times \$3.00/\text{hr} = \$180.00$
    - Department B: $40 \text{ hrs} \times \$2.00/\text{hr} = \$80.00$
    - Department C: $20 \text{ hrs} \times \$5.00/\text{hr} = \$100.00$
    - Total Direct Wages = $\$180.00 + \$80.00 + \$100.00 = \$360.00$
- **Variable Overhead Applied**:
    - Department A: $60 \text{ DLH} \times \$1.00/\text{DLH} = \$60.00$
    - Department B: $40 \text{ DLH} \times \$2.00/\text{DLH} = \$80.00$
    - Department C: $20 \text{ DLH} \times \$4.00/\text{DLH} = \$80.00$
    - Total Variable Overhead = $\$60.00 + \$80.00 + \$80.00 = \$220.00$
- **Fixed Overhead Applied**:
    - Total Labor Hours for Job No. 2390 = $60 + 40 + 20 = 120 \text{ DLH}$
    - Fixed Overhead Applied = $120 \text{ DLH} \times \$2.00/\text{DLH} = \$240.00$
##### 3. Quoted Selling Price Calculation (25% Margin on Selling Price)
Let $S$ represent the prospective selling price. Since profit is required to be 25% of selling price ($0.25 S$), the cost represents 75% of the selling price ($0.75 S$):

$$\text{Selling Price } (S) = \frac{\text{Total Manufacturing Cost}}{1 - \text{Profit Margin Percentage}}$$

$$\text{Selling Price } (S) = \frac{\$4,830}{1 - 0.25} = \frac{\$4,830}{0.75} = \$6,440.00 \quad \text{}$$

$$\text{Profit Component} = \$6,440.00 \times 25\% = \$1,610.00 \quad \text{}$$

### (b)
#### Batch Cost Sheet and Unit Cost Analysis
##### Setup Cost and Production Cost Foundations
In batch costing, setup costs are batch-level fixed costs incurred once per batch regardless of the number of units produced in that batch. Variable production costs vary directly with the number of components produced.
- **Setup Time**: $2 \text{ hours } 20 \text{ minutes} = 2 + \frac{20}{60} = \frac{7}{3} \approx 2.3333 \text{ hours}$.
- **Batch Setup Cost (Fixed per Batch)**:
    - Operator Labor for Setup = $\frac{7}{3} \text{ hrs} \times \$0.72/\text{hr} = \$1.68$
    - Machine Overhead for Setup = $\frac{7}{3} \text{ hrs} \times \$1.50/\text{hr} = \$3.50$
    - **Total Setup Cost per Batch** = $\$1.68 + \$3.50 = \$5.18$
- **Variable Production Cost per Component**:
    - Direct Material per unit = $\$0.0600$
    - Production Time per unit = $10 \text{ minutes} = \frac{10}{60} = \frac{1}{6} \text{ hour}$
    - Operator Labor per unit = $\frac{1}{6} \text{ hr} \times \$0.72/\text{hr} = \$0.1200$
    - Machine Overhead per unit = $\frac{1}{6} \text{ hr} \times \$1.50/\text{hr} = \$0.2500$
    - **Total Production Cost per Component** = $\$0.0600 + \$0.1200 + \$0.2500 = \$0.4300$

> 1. **Profit Margin Base Conversion**: When profit is specified as $25\%$ on selling price ($P = 0.25 S$), the cost-to-selling-price ratio is $75\%$ ($C = 0.75 S$), which translates to a markup of $\frac{25\%}{75\%} = 33 \frac{1}{3}\%$ on total cost.
> 2. **Economies of Scale in Batch Costing**: As batch size increases from 10 to 1,000 units, the fixed setup cost of $\$5.18$ is spread over more units, causing the unit setup cost to drop from $\$0.5180$ to $\$0.0052$ per component, reducing the total unit cost from $\$0.9480$ to $\$0.4352$.
