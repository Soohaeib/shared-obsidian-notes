### (a)
#### Definition of Cost and Cost Accounting
**1. Definition of Cost:** A **cost** is defined as a resource sacrificed or foregone to achieve a specific objective. It is measured as the monetary value given up to acquire goods or services. Costs can be classified as unexpired (assets) when they hold future economic benefits, or expired (expenses) when consumed to generate revenue in the current period.

**2. Definition of Cost Accounting:** **Cost Accounting** is a specialized branch of accounting that measures, analyzes, and reports financial and non-financial information relating to the costs of acquiring or using resources in an organization. It provides detailed cost data to help management plan, evaluate performance, set prices, and maintain operational cost control.
### (b)
#### Difference Between Cost and Expense
While the terms "cost" and "expense" are closely related, they represent distinct stages in accounting:
- **Cost (Unexpired Cost):** Represents the monetary value of resources committed to acquiring an asset or service. As long as the acquired resource retains future economic potential to generate revenue, it is recorded on the Balance Sheet as an asset.
- **Expense (Expired Cost):** Represents the portion of a cost that has been consumed or used up during an accounting period to generate revenue. Expenses are matched against current period revenues on the Income Statement.

|**Basis of Distinction**|**Cost**|**Expense**|
|---|---|---|
|**Fundamental Nature**|Economic sacrifice to acquire an asset or potential value.|Expired cost consumed to produce revenue.|
|**Financial Statement**|Reported on the **Balance Sheet** as an Asset.|Reported on the **Income Statement** as a deduction from Revenue.|
|**Economic Benefit**|Possesses **future** economic benefits.|Benefits have been **realized/exhausted** in the current period.|
|**Example**|Purchase of Tk. 100,000 raw material held in warehouse.|Tk. 60,000 raw material used in products sold this month.|

### (c)
#### Use of Standard Costing and Budgeting in Cost Control
**1. Budgeting in Cost Control:**
- **Target Setting and Planning:** A budget establishes formal, pre-approved spending limits and financial benchmarks for operational departments before operations begin.
- **Resource Allocation:** It prevents unauthorized overspending by requiring departmental commitments to conform to planned master budget targets.
- **Performance Evaluation & Feedback:** Comparing actual monthly operational expenditures with budgeted allowances produces variance reports that highlight operational inefficiencies for prompt management action.

**2. Standard Costing in Cost Control:**
- **Establishing Unit Benchmarks:** Standard costing sets predetermined costs (standards) for direct materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead per unit of output under efficient operating conditions.
- **Variance Analysis:** At the end of a period, actual operational inputs are compared against standard targets to isolate specific variances:
    - _Price/Rate Variances:_ Evaluate purchasing and wage rate performance (e.g., Direct Materials Price Variance).
    - _Efficiency/Quantity Variances:_ Evaluate operational efficiency and material usage (e.g., Direct Labor Efficiency Variance).
- **Management by Exception (MBE):** Managers focus corrective efforts specifically on significant unfavorable variances, allowing smooth daily operations to proceed without unnecessary intervention.
### (d)
#### Definition and Features of Manufacturing Concerns
**1. Definition of Manufacturing Concern:** A **manufacturing concern** is a commercial enterprise that purchases raw materials or components and transforms them into physical finished goods through mechanical, chemical, or manual manufacturing processes involving direct labor and factory overhead.

**2. Features of Manufacturing Concerns:**
- **Transformation/Conversion Process:** Inputs (raw materials) undergo physical or chemical transformation to become distinct market-ready products.
- **Three Stages of Inventory:** Maintains three distinct inventory classifications on the Balance Sheet:
    1. _Raw Materials Inventory:_ Unprocessed materials awaiting production.
    2. _Work-in-Process (WIP) Inventory:_ Partially completed goods undergoing conversion.
    3. _Finished Goods Inventory:_ Completely manufactured units ready for sale.
- **Three Cost Elements:** Production costs are classified into Direct Materials, Direct Labor, and Manufacturing Overhead (indirect materials, indirect labor, plant depreciation, and utilities).
- **Cost Accumulation Systems:** Employs systematic cost accumulation methods (e.g., Job-Order Costing or Process Costing) to determine the Cost of Goods Manufactured (COGM) and Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).
- **High Fixed Capital Requirements:** Requires significant capital investment in fixed production facilities, plant machinery, and factory infrastructure.
