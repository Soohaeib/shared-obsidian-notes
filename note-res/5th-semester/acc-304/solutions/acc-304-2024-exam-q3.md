### (a)
#### Computation of Inventory Stock Levels and Re-order Quantity
#### (a_i)
##### Re-ordering Level
The Re-ordering Level (ROL) represents the inventory quantity at which a fresh purchase order must be initiated to prevent inventory exhaustion prior to delivery.


$$
\text{Re-ordering Level} = \text{Maximum Usage per Day} \times \text{Maximum Lead Time}
$$



$$
\text{Re-ordering Level} = 20 \text{ units/day} \times 15 \text{ days} = 300 \text{ units}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: Re-ordering Level **Re-ordering Level = 300 units**

#### (a_ii)
##### Maximum Level
The Maximum Stock Level is the upper limit of inventory beyond which stock should not be allowed to rise to avoid unnecessary capital lock-up and excessive storage costs.


$$
\text{Maximum Level} = \text{Re-ordering Level} + \text{EOQ} - (\text{Minimum Usage per Day} \times \text{Minimum Lead Time})
$$



$$
\text{Maximum Level} = 300 + 200 - (10 \times 5) = 500 - 50 = 450 \text{ units}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: Maximum Level **Maximum Level = 450 units**

#### (a_iii)
##### Minimum Level
The Minimum Stock Level (or Safety Stock level) serves as a buffer to protect against potential consumption spikes or delivery delays during the lead time period.


$$
\text{Minimum Level} = \text{Re-ordering Level} - (\text{Average Usage per Day} \times \text{Average Lead Time})
$$



$$
\text{Minimum Level} = 300 - (15 \times 10) = 300 - 150 = 150 \text{ units}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: Minimum Level **Minimum Level = 150 units**

#### (a_iv)
##### Danger Level
The Danger Level indicates a critically low stock condition where urgent emergency procurement actions are required to avoid plant shutdown.


$$
\text{Danger Level} = \text{Average Usage per Day} \times \text{Emergency Lead Time}
$$



$$
\text{Danger Level} = 15 \text{ units/day} \times 4 \text{ days} = 60 \text{ units}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: Danger Level **Danger Level = 60 units**

#### (a_v)
##### Re-order Quantity
The Re-order Quantity (Economic Order Quantity) is the optimal order size that minimizes total annual relevant inventory costs.


$$
\text{EOQ} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times U \times O}{S}}
$$


Where:
- $U = \text{Annual Demand} = 5,000 \text{ units}$
- $O = \text{Cost per Order} = \$20$
- $S = \text{Annual Carrying Cost per Unit} = \$5$


$$
\text{EOQ} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 5,000 \times 20}{5}} = \sqrt{\frac{200,000}{5}} = \sqrt{40,000} = 200 \text{ units}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: Re-order Quantity **Re-order Quantity (EOQ) = 200 units**

#### (b_ii)
##### Definition and Key Assumptions of Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) Model
##### Definition of Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)
The Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) is a mathematical decision model that calculates the optimal batch size of inventory to order that minimizes total annual relevant inventory costs (the sum of annual setup/ordering costs and annual inventory holding costs).
##### Key Assumptions of the EOQ Model
1. **Known and Constant Demand:** Annual demand ($D$) for the inventory item is known with certainty and occurs at a constant, uniform rate throughout the accounting period.
2. **Known and Constant Lead Time:** The purchase order lead time (the duration between placing an order and receiving physical delivery) is constant and known with certainty.
3. **Constant Unit Purchase Price:** The purchase cost per unit is constant regardless of the order size (no quantity discounts or price fluctuations occur in standard EOQ).
4. **No Stockouts Allowed:** Stockout costs are infinitely high; inventory is replenished exactly when stock reaches zero so that stockouts never occur.
5. **Instantaneous Delivery:** Order delivery occurs all at once in a single batch (lot) when the reorder point is reached.
6. **Relevant Costs Limited to Ordering and Carrying Costs:** Relevant inventory decisions balance only order placement costs and unit carrying costs.
