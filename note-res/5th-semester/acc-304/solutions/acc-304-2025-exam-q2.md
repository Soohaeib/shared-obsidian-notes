### (a)
#### (a_i)
##### Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)
The Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) minimizes the total annual inventory costs, balancing ordering costs and carrying costs.

$$
\text{EOQ} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times A \times O}{S}}
$$

Where:
- $A = \text{Annual Requirement of Raw Material} = 10,000 \text{ units}$
- $O = \text{Cost of placing an order} = \text{Rs. } 100 \text{ per order}$
- $S = \text{Annual carrying cost per unit} = \text{Purchase Price} \times \text{Carrying Cost \%} = \text{Rs. } 40 \times 20\% = \text{Rs. } 8.00 \text{ per unit per annum}$

**Step-by-Step Calculation:**

$$
\text{EOQ} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 10,000 \times 100}{8}}
$$

$$
\text{EOQ} = \sqrt{\frac{2,000,000}{8}} = \sqrt{250,000} = 500 \text{ units}
$$

> [!success] Final Answer: 500 units The Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) for the raw material is **500 units** per order.

- **Carrying Cost per Unit per Annum under Discount Offer:** $\text{Rs. } 39.20 \times 20\% = \text{Rs. } 7.84$ per unit

$$
\begin{array}{lrr} \hline \textbf{Particulars} & \textbf{EOQ Option (500 Units)} & \textbf{Discount Offer (2,500 Units)} \\ \hline \text{Order Quantity } (Q) & 500 \text{ units} & 2,500 \text{ units} \\ \text{Purchase Price per Unit } (C) & \text{Rs. } 40.00 & \text{Rs. } 39.20 \\ \text{Annual Requirement } (A) & 10,000 \text{ units} & 10,000 \text{ units} \\ \text{Number of Orders per Year } \left(\frac{A}{Q}\right) & 20 \text{ orders} & 4 \text{ orders} \\ \text{Average Inventory } \left(\frac{Q}{2}\right) & 250 \text{ units} & 1,250 \text{ units} \\ \hline \text{1. $$

> [!success] Final Answer: Accept the Offer (Net Savings of Rs. 1,800) The company **should accept** the supplier's discount offer because it reduces total annual inventory costs from **Rs. 404,000** to **Rs. 402,200**, yielding a net annual saving of **Rs. 1,800**.

If carrying cost is maintained on the original base price of $\text{Rs. } 40.00$ ($\text{Rs. } 8.00$ per unit), the total carrying cost for $2,500 \text{ units}$ would be $\text{Rs. } 10,000$, resulting in a total annual cost of $\text{Rs. } 402,400$ and net savings of $\text{Rs. } 1,600$. Under both conventions, accepting the discount offer is advantageous.

#### (a_iii)
##### Re-order Level (ROL)
When maximum consumption rate and maximum lead time are specified, the re-order level is established to prevent stockouts during peak demand periods.

$$
\text{Re-order Level} = \text{Maximum Rate of Consumption} \times \text{Maximum Lead Time}
$$

Where:
- $\text{Maximum Rate of Consumption} = 20 \text{ units per day}$
- $\text{Maximum Lead Time} = 15 \text{ days}$

**Step-by-Step Calculation:**

$$
\text{Re-order Level} = 20 \text{ units/day} \times 15 \text{ days} = 300 \text{ units}
$$

> [!success] Final Answer: 300 units The Re-order Level for this material is **300 units**.

> [!abstract] Working Notes
> **Alternative Formula Verification:**

$$
\text{Re-order Level} = \text{Safety Stock} + (\text{Average Rate of Consumption} \times \text{Average Lead Time})
$$

> - $\text{Average Lead Time} = \frac{5 + 15}{2} = 10 \text{ days}$
> - $\text{Average Consumption Rate} = 15 \text{ units per day}$
> - $\text{Lead Time Consumption} = 15 \text{ units/day} \times 10 \text{ days} = 150 \text{ units}$
> - $\text{Safety Stock} = \text{Re-order Level} - \text{Lead Time Consumption} = 300 - 150 = 150 \text{ units}$
>

##### Competitive Pricing Decision
**Evaluation at 5,000 Flanges:**

If Gayle sells at or below Flora's price of $\$10.00$ per flange while producing $5,000$ units:

$$

$$

> [!success] Final Answer: Pricing Decision **No**, Gayle **cannot** sell below Flora's price of $\$10.00$ and still make a profit at the current volume of $5,000$ flanges. Selling at $\$10.00$ results in an operating loss of **$\$1.40$ per unit** (Total Loss of **$\$7,000$**).

##### Volume Expansion Analysis
Because fixed costs ($\$38,000$) are spread across total output, unit fixed cost drops as volume increases.

$$
\text{Break-Even Volume at Flora's Price } (\$10.00) = \frac{\text{Total Fixed Costs}}{\text{Selling Price} - \text{Variable Cost per Unit}}
$$

$$
X_{BE} = \frac{\$38,000}{\$10.00 - \$3.80} = \frac{\$38,000}{\$6.20} \approx 6,129.03 \text{ units}
$$

If Gayle increases production and sales beyond **$6,130$ units**, total cost per unit drops below $\$10.00$, allowing Gayle to match Flora's price of $\$10.00$ and earn a profit. To undercut Flora's price (e.g., selling at $\$9.50$), Gayle would need a volume of:

$$
X = \frac{\$38,000}{\$9.50 - \$3.80} = \frac{\$38,000}{\$5.70} \approx 6,667 \text{ units}
$$
