# Exceeding 80%: Efficiency % - 80%. If Efficiency <= 80%, bonus = 0.

# For 82%, bonus = 2 * 10 = 20 Taka.

# Let's calculate efficiency for A, B, C:
std_prod = 2000

workers = {'A': 1700, 'B': 1500, 'C': 1900}
for w, act in workers.items():
    eff = (act / std_prod) * 100
    exceeding_pct = max(0, int(eff - 80)) # or float/int? 1700/2000 = 85%, 85 - 80 = 5%
    bonus = exceeding_pct * 10
    piece_pay = act * 0.05
    da = 100
    total = piece_pay + bonus + da
    print(f"Worker {w}: Actual={act}, Efficiency={eff:.2f}%, Exceeding Pct={exceeding_pct}%, Piece Pay={piece_pay}, Bonus={bonus}, DA={da}, Total={total}")

### (a)
#### Definition and Purposes of Time-Keeping
##### Definition of Time-Keeping
Time-keeping in labor cost accounting is the process of preparing an accurate and systematic record of the attendance time and total hours spent by each employee in the factory or workplace.
##### Purposes of Time-Keeping
1. **Calculation of Worker Earnings:** It records the total hours worked by each employee so that basic wages, overtime pay, and gross earnings can be calculated accurately.
2. **Maintenance of Attendance Discipline:** Establishing a formal time-keeping system prevents attendance disputes and avoids dissatisfaction among punctual employees who adhere to working hours.
3. **Record for Terminal and Continuous Benefits:** It provides official service records necessary for determining employee benefits such as pensions, gratuities, paid leave, provident fund contributions, and promotion eligibility.
4. **Apportionment of Overhead Costs:** It supplies accurate direct labor hour data required to apportion indirect manufacturing overhead costs to different jobs or cost centers when labor hours serve as the allocation base.
### (b)
#### Relationship Between Productivity and Labour Costs
Productivity measures the quantitative relationship between actual inputs used (in terms of quantities and costs) and actual outputs produced. Higher productivity is achieved when fewer inputs are used to produce a given volume of output or when a higher output is generated from a given amount of input.

The relationship between productivity and labor costs is inversely correlated and is reflected in the following ways:
1. **Reduction in Direct Labour Cost per Unit:** As labor productivity increases, workers require fewer labor-hours to produce each unit. When wage costs are spread over a higher volume of output, the direct labor cost per unit decreases.
2. **Lower Fixed Overhead Allocation per Unit:** Higher productivity results in greater output within standard working hours. Spreading fixed manufacturing overhead over a larger quantity of produced units reduces the fixed overhead cost and overall conversion cost per unit.
3. **Mutual Financial Advantage Under Incentive Systems:** Under incentive wage schemes, higher productivity allows workers to earn bonus remuneration while simultaneously enabling management to lower total manufacturing costs per unit.
