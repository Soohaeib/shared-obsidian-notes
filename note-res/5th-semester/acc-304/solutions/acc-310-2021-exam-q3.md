### (a)
##### Predetermined Overhead Rate & Job Costing Analysis (Moody Corporation)
#### (a_i)
##### Computation of Predetermined Overhead Rate
To determine the plant-wide predetermined overhead rate (POHR), the total estimated manufacturing overhead cost is first calculated using the fixed overhead cost and the variable overhead rate per machine-hour:


$$
\text{Estimated Total Manufacturing Overhead Cost} = \text{Estimated Fixed MOH} + (\text{Variable MOH Rate per MH} \times \text{Estimated Machine-Hours})
$$



$$
\text{Estimated Total MOH Cost} = \$650,000 + (\$3.00 \times 100,000 \text{ MH}) = \$650,000 + \$300,000 = \$950,000 \quad \text{}
$$


The predetermined overhead rate per machine-hour is computed as follows:


$$
\text{Predetermined Overhead Rate (POHR)} = \frac{\text{Estimated Total Manufacturing Overhead Cost}}{\text{Estimated Total Allocation Base Quantity}}
$$



$$
\text{POHR} = \frac{\$950,000}{100,000 \text{ machine-hours}} = \$9.50 \text{ per machine-hour} \quad \text{}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: Predetermined Overhead Rate = $9.50 per machine-hour

#### (a_ii)
##### Total Manufacturing Cost Assigned to Job 400
The manufacturing overhead applied to Job 400 is calculated by multiplying the actual machine-hours used by Job 400 by the predetermined overhead rate:


$$
\text{Applied Manufacturing Overhead} = \text{Actual Machine-Hours Used} \times \text{POHR}
$$



$$
\text{Applied Overhead}_{\text{Job 400}} = 40 \text{ MH} \times \$9.50/\text{MH} = \$380.00 \quad \text{}
$$


The total manufacturing cost assigned to Job 400 accumulates direct materials, direct labor, and applied manufacturing overhead:


$$
\begin{array}{lr} \hline \textbf{Cost Element} & \textbf{Amount (\$)} \\ \hline \text{Direct Materials} & 450.00 \\ \text{Direct Labor Cost} & 210.00 \\ \text{Manufacturing Overhead Applied } (40 \text{ MH} \times \$9.50/\text{MH}) & 380.00 \\ \hline \textbf{Total Manufacturing Cost Assigned to Job 400} & \underline{\underline{1,040.00}} \\ \hline \end{array}
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: Total Manufacturing Cost Assigned to Job 400 = $1,040.00

#### (a_iii)
##### Manufacturing Overhead Variance & Income Statement Impact
###### 1. Overhead Variance Calculation
- **Actual Manufacturing Overhead Incurred**: $\$1,400,000$
- **Total Applied Manufacturing Overhead**:


$$
\text{Applied MOH} = \text{Actual Total Machine-Hours} \times \text{POHR}
$$



$$
\text{Applied MOH} = 146,000 \text{ MH} \times \$9.50/\text{MH} = \$1,387,000 \quad \text{}
$$



$$
\text{Overhead Variance} = \text{Actual MOH Incurred} - \text{Applied MOH}
$$



$$
\text{Overhead Variance} = \$1,400,000 - \$1,387,000 = \$13,000 \quad (\text{Underapplied Overhead}) \quad \text{}
$$

###### 2. Journal Entry & Impact on Operating Income
- **Closing Entry to Cost of Goods Sold**: Because actual manufacturing overhead exceeds applied manufacturing overhead, overhead is **underapplied** by $\$13,000$. Closing this balance entirely to Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) requires debiting Cost of Goods Sold and crediting Manufacturing Overhead Control:


$$
\begin{array}{llrr} \text{Cost of Goods Sold} & 13,000 & \\ \quad \text{Manufacturing Overhead Control} & & 13,000 \\ \end{array}
$$

- **Impact on Operating Income**: Closing underapplied overhead increases Cost of Goods Sold by $\$13,000$, which **decreases net operating income by $\$13,000$**.

> [!success] Final Answer: Underapplied Overhead = $13,000. Closing this amount to Cost of Goods Sold will decrease operating income by $13,000.
