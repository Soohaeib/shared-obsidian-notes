### (a)
#### Labor Turnover
- **Definition**: Labour turnover is the rate at which employees leave employment at an organization or factory over a specific period relative to the average workforce on the payroll. It includes all departures, whether voluntary resignations or dismissals, regardless of whether the departing employees are replaced.
- **Measurement Methods**:
    1. _Separation Method_:


$$
\text{Labour Turnover Rate} = \frac{\text{All Employees Leaving}}{\text{Average Number Employed}} \times 100
$$

2. _Replacement Method_:


$$
\text{Labour Turnover Rate} = \frac{\text{Number of Replacements in a Period}}{\text{Average Number Employed}} \times 100
$$

3. _Flux Method_:


$$
\text{Labour Turnover Rate} = \frac{\text{All Employees Leaving} + \text{New Employees}}{\text{Average Number Employed}} \times 100
$$

- **Management Relevance**: The separation method is generally preferred as a long-term indicator of labor loss after management has invested in employee training. High turnover negatively impacts training costs, production efficiency, and employee morale.
### (b)
#### IAS-2 (Inventories)
- **Objective & Scope**: Issued by the IASB, International Accounting Standard 2 (IAS 2) prescribes the accounting treatment for inventories, primarily addressing the amount of cost recognized as an asset and carried forward until related revenues are recognized.
- **Definition**: Inventories are assets held for sale in the ordinary course of business, in the process of production for such sale, or in the form of materials/supplies to be consumed in the production process or rendering of services.
- **Measurement Rule**: Inventories must be measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV).
- **Cost Structure**: The cost of inventories comprises all costs of purchase (purchase price, import duties, transport/handling less trade discounts), costs of conversion (direct labor, fixed/variable manufacturing overhead), and other costs incurred in bringing inventories to their present location and condition.
- **Net Realisable Value (NRV)**: The estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business less estimated costs of completion and estimated costs necessary to make the sale.
### (c)
#### Methods of Absorption of Overhead Costs
- **Definition**: Overhead absorption is the process of charging indirect manufacturing costs to cost units or products using an overhead absorption rate based on direct labor hours, machine hours, or direct labor cost.
- **Support Department Overhead Allocation Methods**:
    1. _Direct Method_: Allocates each support department's budgeted costs directly to operating departments only, without allocating costs to other support departments.
    2. _Step-Down (Sequential) Method_: Allocates support-department costs to other support departments and operating departments in a step-by-step directional sequence.
    3. _Reciprocal Method_: Fully incorporates mutual interdepartmental services provided among support departments using simultaneous equations.
- **Year-End Overhead Disposal / Adjustment Methods**:
    1. _Adjusted Allocation-Rate Approach_: Restates all overhead entries in general and subsidiary ledgers at fiscal year-end using actual overhead rates calculated as:


$$
\text{Actual Overhead Rate} = \frac{\text{Total Actual Annual MOH}}{\text{Total Actual Annual Base Quantity}}
$$

2. _Proration Method_: Allocates under- or over-allocated manufacturing overhead among the ending balances of Work-in-Process Control, Finished Goods Control, and Cost of Goods Sold. 3. _Write-Off to Cost of Goods Sold Approach_: Writes off total under- or over-allocated overhead directly to the Cost of Goods Sold account at year-end.
### (d)
#### Product Cost and Period Cost
- **Product Cost (Inventoriable Cost)**:
    - Defined as all costs of a product that an organization regards as assets when incurred.
    - Consists of direct materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead incurred during production.
    - Flows through balance sheet inventory accounts (Direct Materials, Work-in-Process, Finished Goods) and becomes Cost of Goods Sold on the income statement only when the finished units are sold.
- **Period Cost**:
    - Defined as all costs in the income statement other than Cost of Goods Sold.
    - Expensed in the accounting period in which they are incurred because they are expected to generate revenues only in that specific period.
    - Includes all non-manufacturing costs such as R&D, advertising, sales floor labor, customer service, and finished goods warehousing expenses.
### (e)
#### Cost and Expense
- **Cost**:
    - A resource sacrificed or forgone to achieve a specific objective, usually measured as the monetary amount paid to acquire goods or services.
    - Represents an unexpired economic benefit that may be capitalized on the balance sheet as an asset (e.g., equipment purchases, prepaid insurance, or raw materials inventory).
    - Includes both historical actual costs and forecasted budgeted costs.
- **Expense**:
    - An expired cost that has been consumed or used up during the period to generate revenue.
    - Deducted directly from revenues on the income statement in accordance with the matching principle (e.g., Cost of Goods Sold, sales commissions, depreciation expense).
    - _Distinction_: All expenses are expired costs, but costs remain assets on the balance sheet until their economic benefits are exhausted or realized.
