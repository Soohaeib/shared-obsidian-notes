### (a)
#### Inventory Costing Calculations
#### (a_i)
##### First-In, First-Out (FIFO) Costing Method
Under the First-In, First-Out (FIFO) method, the cost of goods sold is charged with the earliest acquisition costs, while the ending inventory is valued using the most recent purchase costs. Under both periodic and perpetual inventory systems, FIFO yields identical results.


$$
\begin{array}{lrr} \hline \textbf{FIFO Inventory Summary} & \textbf{Units / Rate} & \textbf{Amount (\$)} \\ \hline \text{Cost of Goods Available for Sale} & 310 \text{ units} & 5,460.00 \\ \text{Less: Ending Inventory (120 units):} & & \\ \quad \text{Jan 25 Purchase} & 20 \text{ units @ } \$22.00 & 440.00 \\ \quad \text{Jan 15 Purchase (net)} & 50 \text{ units @ } \$20.00 & 1,000.00 \\ \quad \text{Jan 5 Purchase} & 50 \text{ units @ } \$18.00 & 900.00 \\ \hline \textbf{Ending Inventory Valuation} & 120 \text{ units} & \mathbf{2,340.00} \\ \hline \textbf{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)} & & \mathbf{\underline{\underline{3,120.00}}} \\ \hline \end{array}
$$



$$
\text{Gross Profit (FIFO)} = \text{Net Sales Revenue} - \text{COGS} = \$5,680.00 - \$3,120.00 = \$2,560.00
$$


> [!success] Final Answer: FIFO Method
> - **Cost of Goods Sold:** **$3,120.00**
> - **Ending Inventory:** **$2,340.00**
> - **Gross Profit:** **$2,560.00**

#### (a_ii)
##### Moving-Average Costing Method
Under the perpetual moving-average method, a new unit average cost is computed after each purchase or purchase return by dividing the total cost of inventory on hand by the total units on hand. Unit rates are rounded to three decimal places as specified.


$$
\begin{array}{l | rrr | rrr | rrr} \hline \textbf{Date} & \multicolumn{3}{c|}{\textbf{Purchases / Returns}} & \multicolumn{3}{c|}{\textbf{Sales / Returns}} & \multicolumn{3}{c}{\textbf{Balance}} \\ & \text{Qty} & \text{Rate (\$)} & \text{Amt (\$)} & \text{Qty} & \text{Rate (\$)} & \text{Amt (\$)} & \text{Qty} & \text{Rate (\$)} & \text{Amt (\$)} \\ \hline \text{Jan 1} & - & - & - & - & - & - & 100 & 15.000 & 1,500.00 \\ \text{Jan 5} & 140 & 18.00 & 2,520.00 & - & - & - & 240 & 16.750 & 4,020.00 \\ \text{Jan 8} & - & - & - & 110 & 16.750 & 1,842.50 & 130 & 16.750 & 2,177.50 \\ \text{Jan 10} & - & - & - & (10) & 16.750 & (167.50) & 140 & 16.750 & 2,345.00 \\ \text{Jan 15} & 55 & 20.00 & 1,100.00 & - & - & - & 195 & 17.667 & 3,445.00 \\ \text{Jan 16} & (5) & 20.00 & (100.00) & - & - & - & 190 & 17.605 & 3,345.00 \\ \text{Jan 20} & - & - & - & 90 & 17.605 & 1,584.45 & 100 & 17.605 & 1,760.55 \\ \text{Jan 25} & 20 & 22.00 & 440.00 & - & - & - & 120 & 18.338 & 2,200.55 \\ \hline \end{array}
$$

- **Cost of Goods Sold:** $\$1,842.50 - \$167.50 + \$1,584.45 = \$3,259.45$
- **Ending Inventory:** $\$2,200.55$
- **Gross Profit:** $\$5,680.00 - \$3,259.45 = \$2,420.55$

> [!success] Final Answer: Moving-Average Cost Method
> - **Cost of Goods Sold:** **$3,259.45**
> - **Ending Inventory:** **$2,200.55**
> - **Gross Profit:** **$2,420.55**

#### (a_iii)
##### Periodic Weighted-Average Costing Method
Under the periodic weighted-average method, a single weighted-average unit cost is calculated at the end of the accounting period based on all goods available for sale during the month.


$$
\text{Weighted-Average Unit Cost} = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Available for Sale}}{\text{Total Units Available for Sale}} = \frac{\$5,460.00}{310 \text{ units}} = \$17.6129 \approx \$17.613 \text{ per unit}
$$



$$
\begin{array}{lrr} \hline \textbf{Periodic Weighted-Average Summary} & \textbf{Units / Rate} & \textbf{Amount (\$)} \\ \hline \text{Cost of Goods Available for Sale} & 310 \text{ units} & 5,460.00 \\ \text{Less: Ending Inventory} & 120 \text{ units @ } \$17.6129 & 2,113.55 \\ \hline \textbf{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)} & 190 \text{ units} & \mathbf{\underline{\underline{3,346.45}}} \\ \hline \end{array}
$$



$$
\text{Gross Profit (Periodic Weighted-Average)} = \$5,680.00 - \$3,346.45 = \$2,333.55
$$

##### Key Comparative Insights
1. **Impact of Rising Acquisition Costs (Inflation):** Unit acquisition costs increased progressively during January from $\$15.00$ to $\$22.00$.
    - **FIFO** assigns the earliest, lower costs to COGS ($\$3,120.00$) and the latest, higher purchase costs to Ending Inventory ($\$2,340.00$). Consequently, FIFO reports the highest gross profit ($\$2,560.00$) and highest ending inventory value.
    - **Periodic Weighted-Average** averages costs across the entire month, assigning higher average costs to units sold earlier in the month, resulting in the highest COGS ($\$3,346.45$) and the lowest gross profit ($\$2,333.55$).
    - **Moving-Average** updates unit costs continuously after each purchase, reflecting price updates progressively prior to each sale date, yielding intermediate results between FIFO and Periodic Weighted-Average.
2. **Balance Sheet vs. Income Statement Matching:** FIFO provides a more current valuation of ending inventory on the Balance Sheet but fails to match current replacement costs with current sales revenues on the Income Statement. Average cost methods smooth out unit cost fluctuations over time.

##### Halsey Premium Scheme
Under the Halsey Premium Scheme (50% sharing), workers receive their guaranteed basic wages for actual hours worked plus a bonus equal to 50% of the time saved valued at the standard hourly wage rate.
#### (b_ii)
##### Rowan Premium Scheme
Under the Rowan Premium Scheme, bonus is calculated as a proportion of the time saved to standard time allowed applied to basic wages earned for the time taken.


$$
\text{Rowan Bonus} = \left( \frac{\text{Time Saved}}{\text{Standard Time Allowed}} \right) \times \text{Actual Time Taken} \times \text{Hourly Wage Rate}
$$
