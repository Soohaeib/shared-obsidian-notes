### (a)
#### Predetermined Overhead Rates Computation
To calculate the predetermined overhead rate (POHR) for each department, the estimated total manufacturing overhead cost must first be calculated by adding total fixed manufacturing overhead cost to total variable manufacturing overhead cost:

$$\text{Estimated Total Manufacturing Overhead Cost} = \text{Estimated Total Fixed MOH} + (\text{Variable MOH Rate per Unit} \times \text{Estimated Allocation Base Quantity})$$

$$\text{Predetermined Overhead Rate (POHR)} = \frac{\text{Estimated Total Manufacturing Overhead Cost}}{\text{Estimated Total Units in Allocation Base}}$$
##### Cutting Department (Allocation Base: Machine-Hours)
- **Estimated Fixed Overhead**: $\$264,000$
- **Estimated Variable Overhead**: $\$2.00 \text{ per machine-hour} \times 48,000 \text{ machine-hours} = \$96,000$
- **Total Estimated Manufacturing Overhead**: $\$264,000 + \$96,000 = \$360,000$

$$\text{POHR}_{\text{Cutting}} = \frac{\$360,000}{48,000 \text{ MH}} = \$7.50 \text{ per machine-hour}$$
##### Finishing Department (Allocation Base: Direct Labor-Hours)
- **Estimated Fixed Overhead**: $\$366,000$
- **Estimated Variable Overhead**: $\$4.00 \text{ per direct labor-hour} \times 30,000 \text{ direct labor-hours} = \$120,000$
- **Total Estimated Manufacturing Overhead**: $\$366,000 + \$120,000 = \$486,000$

$$\text{POHR}_{\text{Finishing}} = \frac{\$486,000}{30,000 \text{ DLH}} = \$16.20 \text{ per direct labor-hour}$$

$$\begin{array}{lrr} \hline \textbf{Department} & \textbf{POHR Computation Formula} & \textbf{Predetermined Overhead Rate} \\ \hline \text{Cutting} & \frac{\$360,000}{48,000 \text{ Machine-Hours}} & \$7.50 \text{ per machine-hour} \\ \text{Finishing} & \frac{\$486,000}{30,000 \text{ Direct Labor-Hours}} & \$16.20 \text{ per direct labor-hour} \\ \hline \end{array}$$

> [!success] Final Answer: Cutting Department Rate = $7.50 per machine-hour; Finishing Department Rate = $16.20 per direct labor-hour

### (c)
#### Plantwide vs. Departmental Overhead Rate Analysis
Yes, substantially different amounts of overhead cost would be assigned to individual jobs if the company used a single plantwide overhead rate based on direct labor-hours rather than departmental rates.

The rationale for this difference includes:
- **Differing Overhead Cost Drivers**: The Cutting Department is capital-intensive and its overhead is driven primarily by machine usage, whereas the Finishing Department is labor-intensive and its overhead is driven by direct labor activity.
- **Cost Distortion and Cross-Subsidization**: A single plantwide rate based on direct labor-hours would allocate overhead costs across all jobs strictly in proportion to direct labor-hours spent. As a result:
    - Jobs requiring heavy machine usage in the Cutting Department but relatively few direct labor-hours would be allocated very little overhead, leading to significant **under-costing**.
    - Jobs requiring extensive direct labor-hours in the Finishing Department would absorb a disproportionate share of total plant overhead, leading to significant **over-costing**.
- **Improved Accuracy**: Departmental rates accurately capture the true cause-and-effect relationship between overhead cost generation and job consumption across distinct production stages.
