This systematic, step-by-step reading roadmap organizes your studies for **ACC 302: Financial Management**. Standard finance textbooks often present topics in a chronological order that creates circular dependencies (such as introducing bond and stock valuation before the mathematical principles of the Time Value of Money, or presenting Cost of Capital before the Capital Asset Pricing Model).

This learning path is designed with a **strict non-linear progression** to ensure all foundational concepts and mathematical tools are mastered _before_ you proceed to more advanced, applied structures.

---
## Phase 1: Foundations of Financial Management & the Core Mathematical Tool
### 1. Introduction to the Finance Function
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** Topic 1: _An Overview of Financial Management_ (Financial goals of the organisation; Financial management decisions; Agency relationships and resolving conflicts).
- **Logical Dependency:** Establishes the broad responsibilities of the financial manager (investment, financing, and asset management decisions) and the ultimate goal of the firm (maximizing shareholder wealth).
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Class Slides:** _CH-1_OVERVIEW OF FIN MGT.pdf_ (Full Slide Deck).
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):** _Chapter 1: The Role of Managerial Finance_ (Sections 1.1: What is Finance/Goal of the Firm; 1.2: Managerial Finance Function; and 1.3: Organizational Forms, Taxation, and Principal-Agent Relationship).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):** _Chapter 1: The Role of Financial Management_ (What is Financial Management?, The Goal of the Firm, Corporate Governance, and Organization of the Financial Management Function).
### 2. The Core Prerequisite Tool: Time Value of Money (TVM)
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** _Implicit Foundational Prerequisite_ (Required for Topics 2, 3, 4, 6, and 7).
- **Logical Dependency:** **You must master TVM now.** Standard curricula often delay TVM, but you cannot mathematically evaluate bonds, stocks, projects, or cost of capital components without first understanding how to compound and discount cash flows through time.
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):** _Chapter 5: Time Value of Money_ (Sections 5.1: Role of Time Value; 5.2: Single Amounts; 5.3: Annuities; 5.4: Mixed Streams; 5.5: Compounding Frequencies; and 5.6: Loan Amortization).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):** _Chapter 3: The Time Value of Money_ (Simple Interest, Compound Interest, Compounding Frequency, and Amortizing a Loan).

## Phase 2: Valuation of Long-Term Securities (Bonds & Stocks)
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** Partial mapping to Topic 2: _Risk and Returns_ and Topic 6: _Capital Budgeting Decisions_ (Valuation of long-term debt and equity).
- **Logical Dependency:** Understanding how financial assets are priced in the market using TVM discounting must occur _before_ studying required rates of return (Topic 2) and the Cost of Capital (Topic 3).
### 1. Bond Valuation & Interest Rates
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):** _Chapter 6: Interest Rates and Bond Valuation_ (Sections 6.1: Interest Rates; 6.2: Corporate Bonds; and 6.4: Bond Valuation).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):** _Chapter 4: The Valuation of Long-Term Securities_ (Sections: Bond Valuation; Rates of Return or Yields).
### 2. Preferred & Common Stock Valuation
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):** _Chapter 7: Stock Valuation_ (Sections 7.1: Debt vs. Equity; 7.2: Features of Stock; 7.3: Common Stock Valuation; and 7.4: Decision Making and Stock Value).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):** _Chapter 4: The Valuation of Long-Term Securities_ (Sections: Preferred Stock Valuation; Common Stock Valuation).

## Phase 3: Risk and Return (Pricing Market Risk)
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** Topic 2: _Risk and Returns_ (Definition and types of return and risk; Measurement of returns and risk; Portfolio return and risk; The relationship between risk and rate of return; CAPM; Security Market Line; Capital Market Line).
- **Logical Dependency:** Establishes the relationship between risk and investors' required returns. The Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) and Security Market Line (SML) developed here are the exact tools used to determine the Cost of Equity in the next phase.
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Class Slides:** _CH-2_RISK & RETURN.pdf_ (Full Slide Deck: Expected Return, Standard Deviation, Portfolio Risk, CAPM, and SML).
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):** _Chapter 8: Risk and Return_ (Sections 8.1: Risk & Return Fundamentals; 8.2: Risk of a Single Asset; 8.3: Risk of a Portfolio; and 8.4: CAPM/SML).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):** _Chapter 5: Risk and Return_ (Defining Risk & Return, Probability Distributions, Portfolio Risk/Diversification, CAPM, and SML) & _Appendix A: Measuring Portfolio Risk_.

---
## Phase 4: Cost of Capital (Establishing the Hurdle Rate)
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** Topic 3: _Cost of Capital_ (Definition of Cost of Capital; Cost of specific sources of financing—Debt, Preference Capital, Retained Earnings, Newly Issued Common Shares; Weighted Average Cost of Capital—WACC; Marginal Cost of Capital—MCC; Combining MCC and Investment Opportunity Schedule).
- **Logical Dependency:** Blends the required returns of individual debt and equity components into a single composite rate (WACC). This serves as the "hurdle rate" that project returns must clear in Capital Budgeting.
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Class Slides:** _CH-3_COST OF CAPITAL.pdf_ (Full Slide Deck: Component Costs, WACC, and Hurdle Rates).
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):** _Chapter 9: The Cost of Capital_ (Sections 9.1: Overview; 9.2: Cost of Long-Term Debt; 9.3: Cost of Preferred Stock; 9.4: Cost of Common Stock; and 9.5: WACC & Hurdle Rates).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):** _Chapter 15: Required Returns and the Cost of Capital_ (Creation of Value, Overall Cost of Capital, CAPM Approach, and WACC).

---
## Phase 5: Capital Budgeting Decisions (Long-Term Asset Investment)
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** Topic 6: _Capital Budgeting Decisions_ (Concept and importance of Capital Budgeting; Steps in Capital Budgeting; Project classifications; Cash flow estimation; Identifying the relevant cash flows; Capital Budgeting evaluation techniques—PBP, NPV, IRR, PI, MIRR; Capital rationing; Uncertainty in Capital Budgeting).
- **Logical Dependency:** Implements the core investment decision-making tools of the firm. It combines cash flow forecasting with TVM discounting at the project-specific hurdle rate determined in Phase 4.
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):**
        - _Chapter 10: Capital Budgeting Techniques_ (Sections 10.1: Overview; 10.2: Payback Period; 10.3: NPV; 10.4: IRR; and 10.5: NPV vs. IRR / MIRR).
        - _Chapter 11: Capital Budgeting Cash Flows_ (Sections 11.1: Project Cash Flows; 11.2: Initial Cash Flow; 11.3: Periodic Cash Flows; and 11.4: Terminal Cash Flow).
        - _Chapter 12: Risk and Refinements in Capital Budgeting_ (Sections 12.1: Assessing Risk; 12.3: RADRs; and 12.4: Unequal Lives/Capital Rationing).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):**
        - _Chapter 12: Capital Budgeting and Estimating Cash Flows_.
        - _Chapter 13: Capital Budgeting Techniques_ & _Appendix B: Replacement Chain Analysis_.
        - _Chapter 14: Risk and Managerial (Real) Options in Capital Budgeting_ (Total Project Risk, Firm-Portfolio Approach, and Real Options).

---
## Phase 6: Leverage and Capital Structure Decisions
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** Topic 4: _Leverage and Capital Structure_ (Concept and types of Leverage; Measurement of Leverage; Impacts of Leverage; Concept of Capital Structure; Capital Structure Theories; Optimal Capital Structure; Determination of Optimal Capital Structure; EBIT-EPS analysis).
- **Logical Dependency:** Once you know how to invest in assets (Phase 5), you must determine the optimal way to finance them. Capital structure choices alter the firm’s financial risk, which feeds back into the cost of equity (levering/unlevering beta) and WACC.
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):** _Chapter 13: Capital Structure_ (Sections 13.1: Financial Leverage and Its Effects; 13.2: Irrelevance in Perfect Markets; 13.3: M&M and Taxes; 13.4: Trading Off Benefits and Costs; and 13.5: Agency/Asymmetric Info).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):**
        - _Chapter 16: Operating and Financial Leverage_ (Operating/Financial Leverage, EBIT-EPS Indifference Analysis, and Total Firm Risk).
        - _Chapter 17: Capital Structure Determination_ (Net Operating Income Approach, Traditional Approach, M&M, Taxes/Imperfections, Signaling, and Checklist) & _Appendix A: Adjusting Beta for Financial Leverage_.

---
## Phase 7: Dividend Policy (Payout Decisions)
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** Topic 7: _Dividend Policy_ (Basics of dividend and dividend policy; Mechanics of dividend policy; Types of dividend policy; Theories of dividend policy; Factors influencing dividend policy; Stock Dividend, Stock Repurchase, and Stock Split).
- **Logical Dependency:** Evaluates the trade-off between distributing cash to shareholders versus retaining it within the firm. Retained earnings directly affect the common equity component weight of future WACC and the firm's capital structure.
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):** _Chapter 14: Payout Policy_ (Sections 14.1: Basics; 14.2: Payout Irrelevance vs. Relevance; 14.3: Establishing Policy; 14.4: Three Payout Policies; 14.5: Stock Dividends; and 14.6: Stock Splits).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):** _Chapter 18: Dividend Policy_ (Passive vs. Active, Stability Factors, Stock Dividends/Splits, and Stock Repurchases).

---
## Phase 8: Working Capital Management (Short-Term Operations)
- **Syllabus Topic Alignment:** Topic 5: _Working Capital Management_ (Working capital terminology; Requirements for working capital finance; Determining Cash Conversion Cycle; Working Capital estimation; Working Capital investment and financing policy; Cash, Credit, and Inventory Management).
- **Logical Dependency:** Shifts focus from long-term corporate strategy to day-to-day liquidity. Effective working capital management maintains the short-term solvency required for the firm to survive and pursue its long-term strategic investments.
- **Required Reading:**
    - **Smart & Zutter (16e):**
        - _Chapter 15: Working Capital and Current Assets Management_ (Sections 15.1: Net Working Capital Fundamentals; 15.2: Cash Conversion Cycle; 15.3: Inventory Management; 15.4: Accounts Receivable Management; and 15.5: Receipts/Disbursements).
        - _Chapter 16: Current Liabilities Management_ (Sections 16.1: Spontaneous Liabilities; 16.2: Unsecured Short-Term Loans; and 16.3: Secured Short-Term Loans).
    - **Van Horne (13th ed):**
        - _Chapter 8: Overview of Working Capital Management_ (Working Capital Issues, Financing Current Assets, and Combining Decisions).
        - _Chapter 9: Cash and Marketable Securities Management_ (Motives for Holding Cash, Speeding Collections, Slowing Disbursements, and Marketable Securities Portfolio).
        - _Chapter 10: Accounts Receivable and Inventory Management_ (Credit & Collection Policies, Credit Applicant Analysis, and Inventory Control EOQ/Safety Stock).
        - _Chapter 11: Short-Term Financing_ (Spontaneous Financing, Unsecured/Secured negotiated short-term loans, and Factoring Receivables).
