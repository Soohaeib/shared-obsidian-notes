> [!example] Case Scenario
> A newly formed team in a marketing firm is struggling with inefficiencies and poor communication. Despite having a mix of talented individuals, the group is not performing at a high level, and tension has arisen over differing work styles.

### (a)

#### Analysis of the team's current stage within the Stages of Group Development

Based on the provided scenario, the marketing team is currently in the **Storming** stage of the Five-Stage Group Development Model.
The standard stages of group development include:

- **Forming:** Characterized by a great deal of uncertainty about the group’s purpose, structure, and leadership. Members test the waters to determine what types of behaviors are acceptable.
- **Storming:** Characterized by intragroup conflict. Members accept the existence of the group but resist the constraints it imposes on individuality. Conflict arises over who will control the group and how tasks will be executed.
- **Norming:** Close relationships develop and the group demonstrates cohesiveness. There is a strong sense of group identity and camaraderie.
- **Performing:** The structure at this point is fully functional and accepted. Group energy has advanced from getting to know and understand each other to performing the task at hand.
- **Adjourning:** For temporary committees, teams, task forces, and similar groups that have a limited task to perform, this is the stage wrapping up activities and preparing to disband.
  **Justification for the Storming Stage:**
  The team has moved past the initial orientation of the _Forming_ stage as they are actively trying to execute assignments. However, they have run into friction due to interpersonal and functional blockades. The presence of **tension over differing work styles**, **inefficiencies**, and **poor communication** explicitly points to the classic conflict points of the _Storming_ stage. Members are experiencing friction because individual behavioral expectations and work methodologies are clashing before shared expectations and shared norms have been successfully established.

### (b)

#### Action plan for the team leader to transition the group from conflict to high performance

To effectively guide the team out of the _Storming_ stage and move them toward _Norming_ and _Performing_, the team leader should implement the following strategic interventions:

- **Facilitate Open Communication and Conflict Resolution:**
  - The leader must address the interpersonal tension openly rather than ignoring it or hoping it resolves itself.
  - Conduct targeted meetings or facilitated discussions where members can explicitly share their preferred work styles, communication habits, and personal expectations without fear of judgment.
- **Establish Clear Group Norms (Transition to Norming):**
  - Collaboratively define, agree upon, and document the team's standard operating procedures (SOPs).
  - Establish ground rules regarding communication channels (e.g., when to use email vs. instant messaging vs. face-to-face meetings) and expected response turnaround times to eliminate operational inefficiencies.
- **Clarify Roles, Responsibilities, and Project Goals:**
  - Ambiguity often fuels conflict in newly formed teams. The leader must explicitly outline every individual's specific responsibilities and illustrate how their tasks interconnect.
  - Re-emphasize the overarching marketing campaign target or vision to realign individual efforts toward a shared superordinate goal.
- **Adopt a Coaching and Supportive Leadership Style:**
  - During the storming phase, leaders must avoid being purely directive or completely hands-off. They need to actively coach members through relational friction while supporting their technical inputs.

### (c)

#### Strategies to effectively utilize the team's diverse skills and perspectives

To leverage the diverse talents within the marketing team and transform cognitive diversity into a core competitive advantage, the organization should execute the following steps:

- **Align Tasks with Individual Strengths (Complementary Role Assignment):**
  - Map out individual competencies against the demands of the marketing project.
  - Assign tasks strategically based on specific technical strengths: assign analytical profiles to market research and performance data tracking, highly creative profiles to copywriting and visual asset generation, and highly structured profiles to project scheduling and management.
- **Foster Psychological Safety:**
  - Create an inclusive team culture where members feel safe to take interpersonal risks, share divergent ideas, and express unconventional perspectives without fear of negative consequences, ridicule, or rejection.
  - The leader must model this behavior by actively validating unique, non-traditional contributions during team syncs.
- **Implement Structured Decision-Making and Brainstorming Techniques:**
  - To bypass poor communication barriers and clashing styles, utilize structured frameworks such as the **Nominal Group Technique (NGT)** or **Brainwriting**.
  - This ensures introverted or differently styled individuals can submit their perspectives equally without being dominated or drowned out by louder team members.
- **Encourage Cross-Training and Style Appreciation:**
  - Utilize basic behavioral assessments (such as the Big Five personality framework) to help team members understand _why_ their peers approach problems differently.
  - Brief cross-training sessions can help members understand the value of opposing viewpoints—such as why a creative designer needs the structural boundaries provided by a meticulous content strategist, and vice versa.
