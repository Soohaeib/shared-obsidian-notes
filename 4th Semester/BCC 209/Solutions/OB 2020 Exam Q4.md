### (a)

#### The Growing Popularity of Teams in Organizations

The widespread adoption of teams in modern organizations is driven by a structural shift away from traditional, rigid hierarchies toward flexible, dynamic organic structures. The primary factors explaining the growing popularity of teams include:

- **Synergy and Superior Performance:** Teams typically outperform individuals when tasks require a multi-disciplinary approach involving diverse skills, judgments, and experiences. Collective knowledge pooling often leads to more innovative and comprehensive problem-solving than the efforts of a single expert.
- **Flexibility and Responsiveness:** Teams can be assembled, deployed, refocused, and disbanded rapidly. This agility allows organizations to respond effectively to volatile market conditions, technological changes, and competitive pressures.
- **Increased Employee Motivation and Engagement:** Teams facilitate employee participation in operational decisions, granting members greater autonomy. This collaborative environment enhances job satisfaction, fosters organizational commitment, and boosts morale through social support.
- **Workplace Democratization:** By shifting the operational focus away from traditional top-down departmental silos, teams democratize structures. They encourage cross-functional communication and horizontal accountability, which effectively flattens organizational layers.
- **Effective Capability Utilization:** Team structures highlight and combine individual talents more efficiently, ensuring that complex, interdependent tasks are managed by the most qualified group of individuals working in alignment.

### (b)

#### Evaluation Criteria for Choosing Individual Over Team Performance

Teamwork requires significant resource investments, including increased communication overhead, conflict management, and administrative meetings. Organizations can evaluate whether a task is better performed by an individual rather than a team by applying three key diagnostic tests:

1. **Task Complexity and Diversity (Can the work be done better by more than one person?):** If a task is simple, routine, and does not require a variety of perspectives or specialized skills, it is highly efficient to assign it to an individual. Team creation is only justified when the problem is complex enough to require collaborative brainstorming or multi-faceted expertise.
2. **The Presence of a Common Purpose (Does the work create a collective goal that is greater than the sum of individual inputs?):** Teams are designed to work toward a unified, overarching objective. If the objective is merely an aggregate of independent individual goals, organizing a team results in unnecessary process losses. Individual execution is preferred when performance is optimized through isolated accountability.
3. **Interdependence of Members (Are the members of the group mutually reliant?):** Work should be performed by individuals if their activities are sequential or independent. A team approach is preferred only when high reciprocal interdependence exists—meaning the success of the collective output depends directly on the real-time collaboration, coordination, and synchronized contributions of all members.

### (c)

#### Causes and Consequences of Political Behavior in Organizations

Political behavior refers to activities that are not required as part of an individual’s formal organizational role but influence, or attempt to influence, the distribution of advantages and disadvantages within the organization.

#### Causes of Political Behavior

Political behavior stems from an interaction of individual characteristics and organizational environments:

|**Individual Factors**|**Organizational Factors**|
|---|---|
|**High Self-Monitoring:** Individuals sensitive to social cues who possess high capacity to conform behavior to external expectations.|**Reallocation of Resources:** Scarcity or downsizing forces departments to compete, triggering political lobbying for resources.|
|**Internal Locus of Control:** Employees who believe they can manipulate their environment to control their personal outcomes.|**Promotion Opportunities:** Limited upward mobility encourages political maneuvering to gain visibility and favor.|
|**High Machiavellianism:** Personalities characterized by a pragmatic, emotionally detached approach, who believe the end justifies the means.|**Role Ambiguity & Low Trust:** Unclear job boundaries and low-trust cultures allow individuals to engage in politics with low risk of detection.|
|**Organizational Investment & Alternatives:** Individuals with low investment or high external job opportunities are more willing to risk political behavior.|**Performance Evaluation Systems:** Subjective, unclear, or zero-sum reward allocation systems incentivize impression management and political play.|

#### Consequences of Political Behavior

For the vast majority of employees who possess modest political skills or find organizational politics coercive, the consequences are overwhelmingly negative:

- **Decreased Job Satisfaction:** Employees who perceive their workplace as intensely political report lower job satisfaction, viewing resource allocation as unfair and biased.
- **Increased Anxiety and Stress:** Continuous political maneuvering creates a threatening environment, leading to heightened psychological strain and stress among staff.
- **Elevated Turnover Rates:** When organizational politics become pervasive, high-performing employees who prefer merit-based rewards frequently choose to exit the company.
- **Reduced Organizational Performance:** Political behavior can distort communication, subvert organizational goals, and divert critical employee energy away from core task execution, ultimately reducing overall productivity.
