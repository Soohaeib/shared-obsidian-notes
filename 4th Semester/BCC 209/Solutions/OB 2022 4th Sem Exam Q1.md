### (a)

#### What is Organizational Behavior (OB)? Explain in brief why you have been offered a course titled Organizational Behavior (OB) in this Semester. \[4]

**Organizational Behavior (OB)** is a field of study that investigates the impact that individuals, groups, and structure have on behavior within organizations, for the purpose of applying such knowledge toward improving an organization's effectiveness. It is an applied behavioral science built on contributions from a number of behavioral disciplines, such as psychology, social psychology, sociology, and anthropology.

#### Why the Course is Offered in this Semester

The primary reason for offering a dedicated course in Organizational Behavior to business administration students is to transition from purely technical training to interpersonal capability development.

- **Developing Interpersonal Skills:** While technical components (such as accounting, finance, and quantitative methods) are necessary for entering a profession, interpersonal skills or "people skills" determine leadership and managerial success. OB equips students with the structural frameworks needed to manage, communicate with, and lead teams effectively.
- **Understanding Human Complexity:** Human behavior is not random. OB provides a systematic approach to understanding, predicting, and modifying individual and collective human behaviors within institutional structures.
- **Improving Organizational Effectiveness:** Modern managers do not just oversee workflows; they build work cultures. This course provides actionable insights into enhancing job satisfaction, boosting employee productivity, reducing turnover, and encouraging Organizational Citizenship Behavior (OCB).

### (b)

#### Identify and discuss the challenges and opportunities managers have in applying OB concepts. \[7]

The contemporary workplace presents multiple challenges and opportunities that require managers to utilize organizational behavior concepts dynamically. The key areas include:

#### 1. Responding to Economic Pressures

During economic upswings, OB focuses on retention and motivation. Conversely, during economic downturns, managers face tough challenges like downscaling, managing stressed or disgruntled employees, and sustaining morale when job security is low. OB concepts assist managers in implementing difficult decisions while minimizing negative behavioral repercussions.

#### 2. Responding to Globalization

The expansion of global business creates several managerial demands:

- **Increased Foreign Assignments:** Managers must lead teams with different cultural norms, requiring adaptability in leadership and communication styles.
- **Working with People from Different Cultures:** OB concepts help managers decipher how national culture affects motivation, communication, and decision-making styles.
- **Overseeing Movement of Jobs to Countries with Low-Cost Labor:** Managers must navigate the local organizational politics and ethical dilemmas linked with outsourcing and offshoring strategies.

#### 3. Managing Workplace Diversity

Workplace diversity represents a major opportunity to pool varied perspectives, but it presents structural challenges. Managers must pivot from a standardized approach to a differentiated approach that respects diverse demographic variables (including gender, race, age, physical abilities, and values) without permitting systemic discrimination or fracturing group cohesion.

#### 4. Improving Customer Service and People Skills

Most modern jobs are in service sectors where employee behavior directly impacts customer experience. OB provides frameworks to align worker attitudes with organizational goals, ensuring positive interactions that drive customer satisfaction and loyalty.

#### 5. Working in Networked Organizations

The shift toward remote work and digital networks means managers oversee individuals who are geographically dispersed. OB tools help managers build trust, establish clear communication systems, and evaluate performance when direct physical observation is no longer possible.

#### 6. Enhancing Employee Well-Being at Work

Because modern communication technology allows employees to be accessible 24/7, the boundaries between work and personal life have blurred. Managers face the challenge of designing flexible work-life arrangements to avoid employee burnout while maintaining organizational productivity.

#### 7. Creating a Positive Work Environment

Instead of focusing exclusively on correcting employee deficiencies, managers use concepts from **Positive Organizational Scholarship (POS)** to identify and build upon individual and organizational strengths. This approach fosters resilience, vitality, and high-performance potential within teams.

#### 8. Improving Ethical Behavior

Organizations are frequently beset by ethical dilemmas. Managers must foster an ethical climate by modeling integrity, establishing transparent codes of conduct, and creating psychological safety so that employees feel safe reporting unethical actions.

### (c)

#### What is intellectual ability and how is it relevant to OB? Explain in brief. \[4]

**Intellectual ability** refers to the capacity to perform mental activities—such as thinking, reasoning, analyzing, and problem-solving. Unlike physical ability, which focuses on stamina and motor skills, intellectual ability determines cognitive aptitude and capacity.

#### Seven Dimensions of Intellectual Ability

Intellectual ability is composed of several distinct yet interrelated dimensions:

|**Dimension**|**Description**|**Job Example**|
|---|---|---|
|**Number Aptitude**|Ability to do speedy and accurate arithmetic.|Accountant: Computing taxes on a firm's returns.|
|**Verbal Comprehension**|Ability to understand what is read or heard and the relationship of words to each other.|Plant Manager: Following corporate policies on hiring.|
|**Perceptual Speed**|Ability to identify visual similarities and differences quickly and accurately.|Quality Control Inspector: Finding defective parts.|
|**Inductive Reasoning**|Ability to identify a logical sequence in a problem and then solve the problem.|Market Researcher: Forecasting product demand.|
|**Deductive Reasoning**|Ability to use logic and assess the implications of an argument.|Supervisor: Choosing between two employee grievances.|
|**Spatial Visualization**|Ability to imagine how an object would look if its position in space were changed.|Interior Designer: Re-imagining an office layout.|
|**Memory**|Ability to retain and recall past experiences.|Salesperson: Remembering the names of customers.|

#### Relevance to Organizational Behavior

1. **Predicting Job Performance:** Cognitive ability is one of the most reliable and consistent predictors of job performance across various professions. As jobs become more complex and require higher levels of information processing, the demand for strong intellectual abilities increases significantly.
2. **Person-Job Matching (Selection and Placement):** From an operational standpoint, understanding intellectual abilities helps managers design effective recruitment selection tests and place employees in roles that match their cognitive profiles. A mismatch can lead to employee frustration (if the job is too difficult) or severe boredom and disengagement (if the job is under-stimulating).
3. **Structuring Training and Development:** Recognizing the cognitive capacity of employees allows organizations to tailor learning materials and professional development tracks to fit individual learning speeds and logical capabilities, optimizing structural efficiency.
