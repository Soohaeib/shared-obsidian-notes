### (a)

#### Definition, Measurement, and Determinants of Personality

**Definition of Personality**
Personality is defined as the sum total of ways in which an individual reacts to and interacts with others. It is most often described in terms of the measurable traits that a person exhibits.
**How Personality is Measured**
Organizations measure personality to assist in hiring decisions and predict who is best suited for specific organizational roles. The two primary methods of measurement are:

- **Self-Report Surveys:** These are the most common method wherein individuals evaluate themselves on a series of factors or statements. While widely used, a major limitation is that respondents can distort answers to create a favorable impression (faking) or may lack accurate self-awareness.
- **Observer-Rating Surveys:** These surveys are completed by an independent observer, such as a co-worker, manager, or trained psychologist. Research indicates that observer-ratings are better predictors of job performance and success than self-report surveys because they provide an objective, external perspective.
  **Factors that Determine Personality**
  An individual's personality is generally determined by two primary forces:
- **Heredity:** This refers to factors determined at conception. Physical stature, facial attractiveness, gender, temperament, muscle composition, and biological rhythms are largely inherited from parents. Twin studies demonstrate that genetics account for roughly 50 percent of personality differences and more than 30 percent of variation in occupational and leisure interests.
- **Environment:** This includes the culture in which an individual is raised, early conditioning, norms established within family and social groups, and life experiences. The environment acts as a secondary force that shapes and modifies inherited tendencies within specific boundaries.

### (b)

#### Predicting Work Behavior via the Big Five Personality Traits

The Big Five Personality Model identifies five basic dimensions that underlie human personality. Each trait significantly predicts specific work-related behaviors, performance levels, and attitudes:

- **Conscientiousness**
  - _Characteristics:_ Dependable, responsible, organized, and persistent.
  - _Work Behavior Prediction:_ This is the strongest predictor of job performance across almost all occupations. Highly conscientious individuals possess greater job knowledge, exert higher levels of effort, and demonstrate strong organizational citizenship behaviors (OCB). They are significantly less likely to engage in counterproductive work behaviors (CWB).
- **Emotional Stability (Neuroticism)**
  - _Characteristics:_ Calm, self-confident, and secure (high stability) versus nervous, anxious, and insecure (low stability/high neuroticism).
  - _Work Behavior Prediction:_ Strongly predicts job and life satisfaction, as well as lower levels of stress. Emotionally stable individuals are better equipped to cope with high demands, unexpected workplace changes, and high-stress scenarios.
- **Extraversion**
  - _Characteristics:_ Gregarious, assertive, and sociable.
  - _Work Behavior Prediction:_ Predicts success in jobs requiring high interpersonal interaction, particularly sales and managerial roles. Extraverts perform better in leadership positions, are socially dominant, and easily adapt to team dynamics, though they can sometimes exhibit impulsive behaviors.
- **Openness to Experience**
  - _Characteristics:_ Creative, curious, and artistically sensitive.
  - _Work Behavior Prediction:_ Strongly predicts training proficiency and adaptability to organizational change. Open individuals are highly creative, effective at problem-solving in shifting environments, and comfortable with ambiguity.
- **Agreeableness**
  - _Characteristics:_ Cooperative, warm, trusting, and empathetic.
  - _Work Behavior Prediction:_ Predicts performance in customer service and team-oriented environments. Agreeable individuals are highly compliant with organizational rules, effective at conflict resolution, and well-liked, though they may be less successful in strict salary negotiations or highly competitive environments.

### (c)

#### Definition of Perception and Factors Influencing It

**Definition of Perception**
Perception is a process by which individuals organize and interpret their sensory impressions in order to give meaning to their environment. What an individual perceives can be substantially different from objective reality.
**Factors Influencing Perception**
An individual's perception of a target is shaped by a variety of overlapping factors, which are categorized into three main components:

- **Factors in the Perceiver**
  - _Attitudes:_ A person's preconceived notions or biases change how they interpret an event.
  - _Motives:_ Unsatisfied needs or urgent motives strongly focus and distort attention.
  - _Interests:_ Personal preferences draw focus toward specific aspects of an environment while ignoring others.
  - _Experiences:_ Past occurrences create expectations that dictate current interpretations.
  - _Expectations:_ Individuals tend to see what they expect to see.
- **Factors in the Target**
  - _Novelty/Motion:_ Loud noises, bright colors, or moving objects are perceived faster than static ones.
  - _Size/Background:_ The relationship of a target to its background influences how it is isolated and judged.
  - _Proximity/Similarity:_ Elements that are close to one another or look alike are frequently grouped together in the mind of the observer.
- **Factors in the Situation**
  - _Time:_ The temporal context (e.g., time of day) shifts the appropriateness and perception of a behavior.
  - _Work Setting:_ Workplace norms and structural constraints alter how actions are interpreted.
  - _Social Setting:_ The social context or venue where an interaction occurs modifies situational expectations.
