### (a)

#### Concept Statement and Buying Intention Survey

##### Concept Statement for a Campus Restaurant

- **Product/Service:** "Campus Dining Express" is a quick-service restaurant concept located within the university campus zone offering fresh, customizable, and nutrient-dense meals (such as macro bowls, wraps, and cold-pressed juices) tailored for the university community.
- **Target Market:** The primary target market consists of busy university students, faculty members, and administrative staff who require affordable, wholesome meals during tight schedules between classes and lectures.
- **Unique Value Proposition (Benefits):** It resolves the widespread campus issue of limited healthy dining options and long queues by introducing an integrated mobile application for pre-ordering, digital payments, and scheduled pickups.
- **Differentiation:** Unlike standard campus cafeterias or commercial fast-food outlets, "Campus Dining Express" utilizes an automated customization counter and guarantees that orders are ready within five minutes of arrival.
- **Management Team:** Founded by a team of business and hospitality graduates with practical experience in operational logistics and food service management.

##### Conducting a Buying Intention Survey Using the Concept Statement

A buying intention survey is used to gauge initial consumer interest and estimate potential demand. To implement it effectively using the concept statement, the following process is followed:

1. **Concept Exposure:** The finalized one-page concept statement is distributed to a representative sample of the target market (e.g., 50–100 students and faculty members).
2. **Product Description Review:** Respondents read the statement carefully to understand the core features, pricing structures, and operational mechanics of the proposed restaurant.
3. **Administration of the Intention Question:** Respondents are asked a explicit, structured question: _"How likely would you be to purchase meals from this restaurant if it opened near campus?"_
4. **Measurement Scale:** Responses are collected using a standard 5-point scale:
   - Definitely buy
   - Probably buy
   - Might or might not buy
   - Probably not buy
   - Definitely not buy
5. **Data Analysis:** The venture analyzes the combined percentage of the "Definitely buy" and "Probably buy" responses. High percentages within these two categories serve as positive validation of market desirability and help project early revenues.

### (b)

#### Comparison of Business Plans and Red Flags

##### Differences Between a Full Business Plan and an Operational Plan

|**Feature**|**Full Business Plan**|**Operational Plan**|
|---|---|---|
|**Primary Audience**|External stakeholders, including venture capitalists, angel investors, and commercial lenders.|Internal stakeholders, specifically senior executive management, department heads, and operational staff.|
|**Length**|Typically concise, ranging from **25 to 35 pages** in length.|Considerably longer and more detailed, often ranging from **40 to 100+ pages**.|
|**Core Focus**|Strategic overview of the venture, market opportunities, corporate structure, and long-term financial viability.|High-level execution blueprints, daily workflow patterns, production schedules, and internal milestone deadlines.|
|**Main Objective**|To secure external investment capital, bank loans, or strategic corporate alliances.|To serve as an internal operational guide to direct day-to-day activities and tactical coordination.|

##### Red Flags in Writing Business Plans

When external financiers evaluate a business plan, specific errors or omissions serve as immediate warning signs:

1. **Founders with No Financial Skin in the Game:** The creators have not risked any personal capital or assets, which indicates a low level of commitment to the startup's survival.
2. **Broad or Poorly Defined Target Market:** Claiming that the product appeals universally to "everyone" instead of isolating a clearly delineated, measurable market segment.
3. **Overly Optimistic or Aggressive Financial Projections:** Showcasing massive, unrealistic profit spikes (such as classic "hockey-stick" growth trajectories) without providing realistic market-based evidence or sound operational assumptions.
4. **Ignoring or Downplaying Competitors:** Asserting that the business has "no competition," which demonstrates a lack of thorough market research or a failure to recognize indirect market substitutes.
5. **Sloppy Presentation and Structural Errors:** The inclusion of typos, grammatical errors, mathematical miscalculations in financial statements, or disorganized layouts, which implies a lack of professionalism.

### (c)

#### Business Model Innovation

##### Concept of Business Model Innovation: Dell vs. Traditional Manufacturers

Business model innovation represents a fundamental restructure of how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value to secure a distinct competitive advantage within an industry. Based on the provided operational pathways, the contrasting structures demonstrate this innovation:

- **Traditional Manufacturer Model (Build-to-Stock):**
  Companies like Hewlett-Packard or IBM traditionally operate on predictive supply chains. They **forecast demand** $\rightarrow$ **make subcomponents** $\rightarrow$ **assemble complete PCs** $\rightarrow$ **store PCs in warehouses** $\rightarrow$ **ship PCs to retailers** where they sit on store shelves until purchased by a consumer. This framework locks up significant capital in physical inventory, increases warehousing overhead costs, and exposes the firm to product obsolescence risks if market trends shift suddenly.
- **Dell's Innovative Model (Build-to-Order / Direct-to-Consumer):**
  Dell innovated by reversing the entire conventional supply chain sequence. Under their framework, the sequence begins when a **customer places an order via phone or Internet** $\rightarrow$ **contract manufacturers instantly view order info and ship component parts** $\rightarrow$ **Dell assembles the computer from components as they arrive** $\rightarrow$ the **computer is shipped "direct" to the customer via UPS or FedEx** into the hands of the consumer.

##### Key Elements of Dell's Business Model Innovation

1. **Disintermediation (Direct-to-Consumer):** By bypassing third-party wholesalers and brick-and-mortar retail networks, Dell retains higher margins and establishes a direct relationship with its consumer base.
2. **Just-In-Time (JIT) Inventory Control:** Dell eliminates finished-goods warehouse storage. Components are sourced and assembled on-demand, lowering holding costs and reducing inventory depreciation.
3. **Negative Cash Conversion Cycle:** Dell collects electronic payments from buyers _before_ components are assembled or paid out to suppliers, providing superior working capital advantages over legacy manufacturers.
