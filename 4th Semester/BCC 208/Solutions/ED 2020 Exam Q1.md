### (a)

#### Definition of Entrepreneur and Entrepreneurship

- **Entrepreneur**: An entrepreneur is an individual who identifies a market need, conceives an innovative idea, mobilizes the necessary resources, and takes the calculated risks to launch and manage a new venture. The entrepreneur acts as a catalyst for economic change by introducing new products, services, or production processes, bearing the primary financial and operational risks while reaping the potential rewards.
- **Entrepreneurship**: Entrepreneurship is the dynamic process of conceptualizing, organizing, developing, launching, and managing a new business venture. It involves transforming a creative idea into a viable economic entity, managing structural uncertainties, navigating risks, and driving value creation through innovation and strategic execution.

### (b)

#### Major Tasks of an Entrepreneur

An entrepreneur performs several interconnected tasks to transform an abstract concept into an ongoing business enterprise. The primary tasks include:

1. **Opportunity Identification and Evaluation**: Scanning the environment to spot unmet market needs, technological shifts, or gaps in existing solutions, and assessing whether these opportunities are commercially viable.
2. **Developing a Business Plan**: Formulating a comprehensive strategic blueprint that maps out the venture's target market, value proposition, operational structure, marketing approach, and financial projections.
3. **Resource Mobilization**: Gathering and organizing essential assets, including financial capital from investors or lenders, raw materials, intellectual properties, and physical facilities required for initial operations.
4. **Building the New Venture Team**: Recruiting, organizing, and leading a capable management and operational team to execute the business model effectively.
5. **Risk Management**: Identifying potential strategic, financial, operational, and legal risks, and designing mitigation strategies to safeguard the venture during its early stages.
6. **Innovation and Value Creation**: Continuously refining products, processes, or business models to remain competitive and adapt to evolving customer expectations.

### (c)

#### Essential Characteristics of an Entrepreneur

Successful entrepreneurs exhibit a distinct blend of psychological traits, skills, and behaviors that enable them to navigate the challenges of launching new ventures:

- **Passion for the Business**: An intense commitment and belief in the venture’s mission, which serves as the primary driver to sustain hard work and overcome systemic obstacles.
- **Product/Customer Focus**: A deep understanding of and obsession with delivering true value to customers, ensuring that the product or service addresses genuine pain points.
- **Tenacity Despite Failure**: The resilience to bounce back from setbacks, rejections, and operational failures, treating them as critical learning points rather than terminal roadblocks.
- **Execution Intelligence**: The ability to effectively implement strategies, make decisive choices under pressure, and translate creative concepts into concrete operational realities.
- **Calculated Risk-Taking Propensity**: The willingness to accept business risks, balanced by systematic evaluation and planning to minimize negative outcomes, rather than engaging in blind gambling.

### (d)

#### Differentiation Between Entrepreneur and Intrapreneur

While both roles focus on innovation and value creation, they operate within fundamentally different environments and risk structures:

|**Feature / Basis**|**Entrepreneur**|**Intrapreneur**|
|---|---|---|
|**Definition**|An independent individual who builds a new business from scratch.|An employee within an established corporate entity who drives internal innovation.|
|**Capital & Resources**|Must independently raise and mobilize financial, human, and material capital.|Utilizes the existing corporate resources, infrastructure, and budget of the employer.|
|**Risk Exposure**|Bears total personal, financial, and professional risk if the venture fails.|Faces minimal personal financial risk; risks are absorbed by the corporation.|
|**Autonomy**|Operates with complete independence and sovereign decision-making power.|Subject to organizational hierarchy, corporate policies, and management approvals.|
|**Reward Structure**|Receives the direct profits and equity value generated by the business.|Typically rewarded with fixed salary, performance bonuses, or corporate recognition.|
