### (a)

#### Balance of Payments of Bangladesh

The Balance of Payments (BOP) is a comprehensive and systematic statistical record of all economic transactions between the residents of Bangladesh and the rest of the world over a specific period, typically a financial year. It reflects the country's external economic health and international financial position.

The structure of Bangladesh's Balance of Payments is broadly classified into three main accounts:

- **Current Account:** This is the most crucial component of Bangladesh's BOP and consists of:
  - _Trade Balance:_ Records the export and import of goods. Bangladesh historically experiences a structural trade deficit because its import payments (for capital machinery, petroleum, intermediate goods, and industrial raw materials) consistently exceed its export earnings (heavily dominated by Ready-Made Garments).
  - _Services Account:_ Includes net receipts and payments for transport, travel, insurance, and intellectual property. This account traditionally remains in deficit.
  - _Primary Income:_ Records institutional compensation of employees and investment income (interest, dividends).
  - _Secondary Income (Remittances):_ This represents current transfers, primarily containing inward remittances sent by Bangladeshi expatriates working abroad. Remittances act as the backbone of Bangladesh's external economy, often offsetting the massive trade deficit and stabilizing the current account balance.
- **Capital Account:** Tracks capital transfers, such as foreign debt forgiveness and the acquisition or disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets. This account generally registers small, positive volumes.
- **Financial Account:** Tracks net flows of financial assets and liabilities. It includes:
  - _Foreign Direct Investment (FDI):_ Net investments in physical capital and enterprises.
  - _Portfolio Investment:_ Investments in shares, bonds, and financial derivatives.
  - _Other Investments:_ Includes short-term and long-term official foreign loans, trade credits, and assistance from multilateral agencies (World Bank, IMF, ADB).

The net summation of the Current, Capital, and Financial Accounts plus **Errors and Omissions** determines the **Overall Balance**. A positive overall balance increases Bangladesh Bank's foreign exchange reserves, whereas a negative balance depletes them, putting pressure on the exchange rate of the Bangladeshi Taka (BDT).

### (b)

#### The 8th Five Year Plan of Bangladesh

The 8th Five Year Plan (FY2021 – FY2025) of Bangladesh, themed **"Promoting Prosperity and Fostering Inclusiveness,"** was formulated to steer the country through critical development milestones, including the post-COVID-19 macroeconomic recovery, acceleration of economic growth, and structural alignment for graduation from the Least Developed Country (LDC) status.

The core strategies and objectives of the 8th Five Year Plan include:

- **Macroeconomic Targets and GDP Growth:** The plan originally targeted an ambitious average GDP growth rate acceleration up to 8.51% by the terminal year (FY2025). It prioritizes domestic resource mobilization by expanding the tax-to-GDP ratio and maintaining stable inflation.
- **Poverty and Inequality Reduction:** It set specific targets to bring down the national head-count poverty rate to 15.6% and extreme poverty to 7.4% by 2025. The strategy relies on broadening the Social Safety Net Programs (SSNPs) and improving their targeting efficiency.
- **Employment Generation:** The plan focuses on generating approximately 11.3 million domestic and overseas jobs. This is designed to harness the country’s "demographic dividend" by shifting excess labor from low-productivity agriculture to high-productivity, labor-intensive export manufacturing sectors.
- **Human Capital Development:** Allocations are prioritized for health, education, and technical skills development (TVET) to build a knowledgeable workforce capable of adapting to the Fourth Industrial Revolution (4IR).
- **Infrastructure and Environmental Sustainability:** The plan drives the completion of national mega-projects (such as the Padma Bridge, Metro Rail, Deep Sea Ports, and Fast-Track Economic Zones). Furthermore, it integrates sustainable environmental practices by aligning national targets with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the Bangladesh Delta Plan 2100.

### (c)

#### Poverty Alleviation Programs of Different NGOs in Bangladesh

Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs) have historically played a pioneering, transformative role alongside the government in reducing poverty across rural and urban Bangladesh. Rather than relying solely on relief distribution, prominent NGOs deploy targeted socio-economic development frameworks:

- **BRAC (Building Resources Across Communities):**
  - _Ultra-Poor Graduation Program (TUP):_ A globally acclaimed model that selects the poorest households and provides them with holistic assistance, including free asset transfers (livestock or land), enterprise training, health support, and intensive mentoring to pull them out of extreme poverty.
  - _Microfinance and Social Enterprises:_ Operating targeted credit programs that grant small, collateral-free loans to poor women, empowering them to start small-scale agricultural or poultry businesses.
- **Grameen Bank:**
  - _Microcredit Program:_ Pioneered the group-based peer-lending methodology targeting rural women. Loans are extended for income-generating activities, allowing borrowers to establish self-employment options.
  - _Struggling Members (Beggar) Program:_ An initiative targeting the absolute poorest by providing interest-free, flexible loans for micro-trading (selling small household items), coupled with free life insurance.
- **ASA (Association for Social Advancement):**
  - _Cost-Effective Microfinance:_ Known for its highly efficient operational model, ASA provides flexible microloans, small-and-medium enterprise (SME) loans, and micro-insurance programs to low-income populations to mitigate financial shocks.
- **TMSS (Thengamara Mohila Sabuj Sangha):**
  - _Northern Region Upliftment:_ Concentrated significantly on the poverty-stricken areas of Northern Bangladesh, TMSS utilizes a combination of microcredit, primary healthcare services, and agricultural skill training to improve the livelihoods of destitute rural women.
- **Proshika:**
  - _Social Forestry and Human Development:_ Focuses on employment generation through eco-friendly initiatives, human rights training, and participatory field-level projects such as organic farming and small-scale cattle breeding.
