Based on the provided sources, the following identifies and explains the types of risk present in each of the seven scenarios within the context of the Bangladesh economy:

# (a)

#### Terrorist Attack Alert

- **Type of Risk:** **Nondiversifiable (Fundamental) Risk** and **Pure Risk**.
- **Explanation:** This is a **nondiversifiable or fundamental risk** because a terrorist attack affects the entire economy or large numbers of persons or groups within the nation simultaneously. It is also a **pure risk** because the only possible outcomes are adverse (a loss occurs) or neutral (no loss occurs); there is no potential for profit from a terrorist attack. In modern risk management, terrorism is also classified as a significant **foreign loss exposure** or **emerging risk**.

# (b)

#### House Damaged in a Fire

- **Type of Risk:** **Property Risk** (specifically **Direct Loss**) and **Diversifiable (Particular) Risk**.
- **Explanation:** This is a **property risk** involving a **direct loss**, which is the financial loss resulting from the actual physical damage to the structure. It is categorized as a **diversifiable (or particular) risk** because it affects only individuals or small groups rather than the entire economy and can be managed through diversification or insurance. It is a **pure risk** because there is only the possibility of loss or no loss.

# (c)

#### Total Disability of a Family Head in a Plant Explosion

- **Type of Risk:** **Personal Risk** and **Pure Risk**.
- **Explanation:** This is a **personal risk** because it directly affects an individual and their family by involving the possibility of the loss or reduction of earned income and the depletion of financial assets. It is also a **pure risk** because the outcome is either a loss (disability) or no loss (remaining healthy). In terms of impact, it is generally considered a **diversifiable risk** as it affects a specific family unit.

# (d)

#### Purchase of 100 Shares of National Life Insurance Stock

- **Type of Risk:** **Speculative Risk**.
- **Explanation:** Investing in common stock is a classic example of **speculative risk** because there is a clear possibility of **either profit or loss**. The investor will profit if the price of the National Life Insurance stock increases but will suffer a financial loss if the price declines.

# (e)

#### River Overflowing (Flood) causing Damage in a Floodplain

- **Type of Risk:** **Nondiversifiable (Fundamental) Risk** and **Pure Risk**.
- **Explanation:** Natural disasters such as widespread flooding are **nondiversifiable or fundamental risks** because they affect large numbers of persons or groups within a region or economy at the same time. Because these risks are difficult to manage privately, they often require government assistance, such as specialized flood insurance programs. It is a **pure risk** because the only outcomes are damage (loss) or no damage (no loss).

# (f)

#### Higher Mortgage Payments Due to Interest Rate Hikes

- **Type of Risk:** **Financial Risk** and **Nondiversifiable Risk**.
- **Explanation:** This is a **financial risk**, which refers to the uncertainty of loss because of adverse changes in **interest rates**, commodity prices, or foreign exchange rates. Because a decision by the Bangladesh Bank to raise interest rates would affect the entire economy and large numbers of home buyers simultaneously, it is also classified as a **nondiversifiable (fundamental) risk** that cannot be eliminated through individual diversification.

# (g)

#### Playing Slot Machines in a Casino

- **Type of Risk:** **Speculative Risk** (specifically **Gambling**).
- **Explanation:** This is a **speculative risk** because both profit and loss are possible outcomes. In the sources, gambling is specifically distinguished from insurance: while insurance deals with existing pure risks (like fire), gambling **creates a new speculative risk** for the purpose of play or gain. Unlike insurance, gambling is often viewed as socially unproductive because the winner's gain comes at the direct expense of the loser.
