### (a)

#### Fire policies

Fire policies are legal contracts between an insurer and an insured where the insurer agrees to indemnify the insured against financial loss or damage caused by fire or other specified perils to the insured property (such as buildings, machinery, stock, or fixtures) during a specific period. The indemnity is limited to the actual cash value of the loss or the sum insured, whichever is less.

### (b)

#### Fire policies condition

Fire policies conditions are the explicit rules, terms, and requirements specified in the insurance contract that both parties must fulfill. These include conditions precedent to liability, such as the requirement of utmost good faith, maintaining proper descriptions of the property, notifying the insurer immediately in the event of a fire, taking steps to minimize loss, and cooperating with the claims investigation process. Breach of these conditions can void the policy.

### (c)

#### Marine losses

Marine losses refer to the destruction, damage, or financial loss of cargo, hull (ship), or freight during transit over waterways due to maritime perils. These are broadly classified into:

- **Total Loss:** Can be Actual Total Loss (where the property is completely destroyed or irretrievably lost) or Constructive Total Loss (where the cost of recovering/repairing the property exceeds its value).
- **Partial Loss:** Can be Particular Average (an accidental partial loss borne entirely by the specific owner) or General Average (a deliberate sacrifice made for the common safety of all interests, with losses shared proportionately among all parties involved).

### (d)

#### Marine perils

Marine perils refer to the hazards and risks associated with navigation and maritime transport that are covered under a marine insurance policy. These include "perils of the sea" such as bad weather, collisions, stranding, sinking, waves, and sinking, as well as "perils on the sea" which include fire, piracy, theft, jettison (throwing cargo overboard to save the ship), and barratry (wrongful acts committed by the master or crew).

### (e)

#### Doctrine of Subrogation

The Doctrine of Subrogation is a legal principle arising from the principle of indemnity. It states that after an insurer has fully compensated the insured for a loss, the insurer acquires all the legal rights and remedies that the insured possesses against any third party who caused the loss. This prevents the insured from collecting double recovery for a single loss and ensures that the negligent party is held responsible.

### (f)

#### Re-insurance

Re-insurance is an arrangement where one insurance company (the ceding insurer) transfers or passes on a portion of its risk exposure to another insurance company (the reinsurer). This allows the primary insurer to protect itself against catastrophic losses, stabilize its financial position, increase its capacity to underwrite larger risks, and maintain solvency by shifting risks that exceed its retention limit.

### (g)

#### Valid contract

A valid contract in insurance is an agreement that satisfies all general legal requirements of the law of contracts and specific insurance principles, **making it legally binding and enforceable in a court of law**. The essential elements include offer and acceptance, lawful consideration, capacity of parties, free consent, and legal object, along with specific insurance components such as insurable interest, utmost good faith, and indemnity.
